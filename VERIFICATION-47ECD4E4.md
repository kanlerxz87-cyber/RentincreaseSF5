## 2. Licence question — verified again, step-by-step for a beginner

**Question:** *Do I, as an individual owner (not a corporation), need to inform the Rent Board and apply for a licence before notifying tenants of a rent increase?*

**Short answer:** **Licence yes; application no.** This is documented in O1 + O3.

**Step-by-step (do this before serving any notice):**

1. **Confirm you are covered.** O3 §37.15(a) says **“owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37”** shall report — it lists no threshold by entity form, number of units, or corporation vs individual. A two-unit 1940 building is the right vintage for coverage (O11: on/before June 13, 1979 *may* be covered), but coverage still needs checking on the Property Information Map and lease — the 1940 date is owner-supplied and flagged.
2. **Create a Rent Board Portal account and report both units into the Housing Inventory.** O1: reporting is **free, online 24/7**, required to be updated **by March 1 each year** and within 30 days of owner/manager contact change. You report mailing address, occupancy, manager contact, approx. sq ft in 250-sq-ft increments, bedrooms/bathrooms, dates, and base rent in $250 increments — all **under penalty of perjury** and **public** (O1, O3). Do not paste tenant names, initials or free-text identifiers; the form does not need them.
3. **Understand the licence is automatic, not an approval.** O1: *“Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase licence”* and *“a rent increase licence will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.”* There is no licence application form and no waiting period for ordinary increases. **No petition or prior approval** is needed for an ordinary annual or properly calculated banked increase (O2, O4 §37.3, O6).
4. **Check the licence is current *before* you serve.** O1 + O3 §37.15(e): licence must be **current on the effective date** of the increase or the increase is **not enforceable** during suspension; late reporting restores the licence **prospectively only** — you cannot retroactively collect the increase for suspended months. The pages do not state the licence must already exist at the moment of service; obtaining it before service is the **safer workflow** taught on the site (F20), not a separately quoted legal prerequisite.
5. **Then serve the notice correctly** (see §5 below). The licence **does not need to be attached** to the notice (O1) and does **not** shield you from other challenges (cap, discrimination, retaliation, harassment).

**Official links to keep open while you work:** [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [§37.15 text](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · Portal at [portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/).

## 5. Correct way to inform tenants + reasonable reasons

### Notice checklist (verified O2 + O7 + O8)

- [ ] Written notice, in English + required translations where applicable.
- [ ] States **dollar amount**, **percentage**, and **effective date** (O2).
- [ ] For banked increases, states **which portion is banked and the dates it is based on** (O7 Rule 4.12(b) — omission alone does not void, but including it is required).
- [ ] **30 days** for ≤10% or **90 days** for >10% (including combined increases in prior 12 months) — O8 §827(b)(2)–(3).
- [ ] **+5 days if mailed** (O2); **personal delivery** otherwise under CCP §1013.
- [ ] Served to **all tenants / occupants**; retain proof of service; keep copies.

### What counts as a reasonable *reason*

The **lawful allowance (1.6%) needs no extra justification** — it is the cap the Board publishes. Do **not** cite repair complaints, personal disputes, or invented business hardship as a reason. Valid contextual facts (not legal permission to exceed the cap) include: rising operating costs, insurance, utilities, maintenance backlog — explained neutrally, without claiming they change the legal maximum. Any “hardship” petition is a **separate filing** (O3–O4 context) with its own rules, not a line in the annual notice.

### Tenant-friendly scrutiny — will it pass a SF tenant lawyer’s review?

San Francisco provides **free tenant services** (O2 context + Form 1007 guidance; Rent Board counsellors 415-252-4600). A tenant lawyer will ask:

1. **Licence?** Is the Inventory current and licence live on the effective date? (O1, O3).
2. **Coverage & base rent?** Is the unit actually covered? Is the base rent lawful and fully documented? (O11, O2).
3. **Math?** Is the percentage from the correct Form 571 period (effective date)? Any compounding or rounding up? Banking eligible? (O5, O6–O7).
4. **Notice?** Was the correct 30/90-day period + mailing rule met, with all required contents and proper service? (O8, O2).
5. **Motive?** Is there evidence of **discrimination** (O14, O15), **retaliation** (O13: 180-day bar; organizing bar with no limit), or **harassment** (O12 context: coercion to vacate, treble damages)?

**No project amount is certified to pass.** Meeting the cap does not immunize a notice that is late-licensed, mis-served, retaliatory or discriminatory. The site’s *Tenant-lawyer test* page is a **checklist**, not certification, and flags unresolved facts (F01–F03, F05, F18).

## 6. One unit or both — discrimination inquiry

**No provision requires identical or simultaneous increases across units.** The reviewed ordinance and rules (O3, O4) were searched for such a rule — none was found, recorded as **no-authority-found** (R52), not inferred.

That **does not** make a selective increase automatically lawful. A tenant-side inquiry will examine:

- Were tenants selected based on a **protected characteristic or activity** (O14 FEHA: race, national origin, immigration status, primary language, source of income, familial status, disability, etc.; O15 federal; O13 retaliation)? Raising **both** units is not automatic protection if motive is otherwise unlawful; raising **only one** demands a factual review of motive, timing relative to complaints/organizing, and accommodation duties.
- Was the **same written method** applied to both (same calculation rule, same service proof, same record-keeping)?
- Is there **contemporaneous documentation** predating any dispute?

Practical risk management (not legal advice): apply one written policy to both units; document the calculation identically; serve the same way; keep the same evidence packet. The project does **not** recommend raising one vs. both — it records the absence of a uniformity rule and the discrimination/retaliation limits.

## 7. Local / state / federal layers — which cap controls

- **Local (SF):** **1.6%** controls for confirmed rent-controlled units (O2, O4, O5). The **Rent Board Fee** is **$59/unit for 2026–27** due March 1 (O12); fee exemption ≠ inventory compliance.
- **State (CA AB 1482):** **8.8%** for **8/1/2026–7/31/2027** on *state-covered* units only — O10 explicitly states it **does NOT apply** to SF Rent Ordinance units. No state permission to exceed the local cap.
- **Federal:** **No general rent ceiling** for private unsubsidized housing identified (O15 review). Federal law here is **anti-discrimination** (Fair Housing Act: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability), plus any subsidy conditions not supplied.
