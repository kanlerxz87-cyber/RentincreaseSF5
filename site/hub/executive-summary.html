Executive summary

Prepared 7 November 2026; every official source re-read on 8 November 2026. The licence question is answered first because nothing else can be done safely until it is settled.

1. The licence question

You must be licensed, but you do not apply. Report each unit into the Rent Board Housing Inventory; a rent increase licence is generated automatically for a tenant-occupied unit. It must be current on the effective date of the increase. Individual owners are covered exactly as corporations are. S1 S2

No petition or prior approval is needed for an annual or banked increase. S3 S13

2. What is a normal increase here

For a rent-controlled unit there is no market test. The lawful annual increase is the norm: 1.6% for effective dates from 1 March 2026 through 28 February 2027, once every 12 months, on base rent only, never rounded up. S4 S5 The statewide 8.8% figure does not apply to these units because they are already under a stricter local ordinance. S9 S10

3. The banking wording, and why it does not change your numbers

The Rent Board's banking page says a skipped increase banks only if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. Rule 4.12 says a full 12 months must have elapsed from the date the increase could have been imposed. The Board's time-stamped worked examples follow the rule, not that sentence: on the banking page a 1.7% increase available on 1 August 2024 is banked on 1 August 2025, twelve months later; on the rent increase page a 1.4% increase is permanently lost eleven months after it became available, even though 35 months had passed since the tenant moved in. S6 S7 S5

Practical effect for you: none. Unit 1 reaches twelve months after its skipped increase, and twenty-four months after the tenancy began, on the same date: 1 February 2027. Unit 2 has had no increase since 2019. Both readings therefore give the same answer for both units, so the 3.0% figure does not depend on resolving the wording. The conflict is published as F04 on the flagged page; ask a counsellor in writing if you want it closed.

4. One unit or both

No provision requiring identical or simultaneous increases across units was found in the sources reviewed. S3 What the law prohibits is a difference driven by a protected characteristic S18 S19 or a retaliatory or coercive purpose. S15 S17 The two tenancies are in materially different positions - one began in 2025, the other in 2019 with years of unused increases - so treating them differently is explained by the tenancy history, not by the tenants. Document that reasoning in writing before serving anything.

5. What to do next

  1. Confirm the Inventory and licence

    Check the public Rent Board Portal for both units. If either is unreported, report it now - it is free and the licence is generated on submission. S1

  2. Confirm coverage

    Verify the 1940 construction date on the Property Information Map. Coverage follows the pre-14 June 1979 rule. S11

  3. Fix the effective dates

    Unit 1: 1 February 2027 keeps the anniversary. Unit 2: on or before 28 February 2027 keeps the published 1.6% annual rate. S4 S5

  4. Choose the size

    Decide between the low-scrutiny annual-only option and the full banked amount. Unit 2 above 10% needs 90 days. S8

  5. Write the file note

    Record the tenancy history, the arithmetic and the business reason. This is what a tenant-side review will ask for. S20

  6. Serve and keep proof

    Serve personally or by mail with the extra 5 days, and keep the proof of service and a copy of the notice. S8 S5
