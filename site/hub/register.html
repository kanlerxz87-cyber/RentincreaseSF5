R01Does an individual owner (not a corporation) have to report?Yes. Sec. 37.15(a) requires 'owners of residential dwelling units' to report. No entity-type or portfolio-size exemption appears in the text.VerifiedS2 S1
R02Is there a licence application to file or an approval to wait for?
Widely repeated online claims of a licence 'application' are not supported by the official page.
No application. The licence is generated automatically once the unit is reported as non-owner-occupied in the Housing Inventory.VerifiedS1
R03Must the licence exist before the increase takes effect?Yes. A current rent increase licence must be on file before any annual or banked increase goes into effect; without it the notice is not enforceable.VerifiedS1 S2
R04What happens if the owner has not reported?
Sec. 37.15(e). This is not retroactive - late reporting does not revive past months.
The licence is suspended. On reporting, it is restored prospectively only; the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the suspension.VerifiedS2
R05When did reporting start for a building of this size?March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. July 1, 2022 applied only to non-condo buildings with 10 or more units.VerifiedS2 S1
R06Annual reporting deadlineMarch 1 each year.VerifiedS1 S2
R07Penalty for reporting after the deadlineThe Rent Board does not assess monetary penalties for late Housing Inventory filing. A submission after the deadline still generates a licence.VerifiedS1
R08Must the licence be attached to the rent increase notice?No. The licence does not need to be included with the notice.VerifiedS1
R09Cost and method of reportingFree, online through the Rent Board Portal, available 24/7. An account is required.VerifiedS1

Privacy consequence: do not put tenant names in free-text fields; the portal asks for owner business contact details, occupancy dates and base rent in $250 increments.
No. Information entered in the Housing Inventory submission is public.VerifiedS1

R27Official worked example settles the measuring pointTenant moved in 6/1/2023; increase imposed 5/1/2026. The 1.7% available 6/1/2024 IS banked; the 1.4% available 6/1/2025 is NOT, and is permanently lost. The clock runs 12 months from the date each increase became available.VerifiedS5

Interpretation, not a quoted rule. See the Flagged page. It does not change either unit's number.
