RENT-CONTROLLED HOUSING

License & notice

An evidence-led sequence for an ordinary annual increase.

Question / checkpointFindingEvidence
01 · Confirm coverage and leaseEstablish the applicable rent-control regime, lawful base rent, original move-in, full increase history and current lease restrictions. Do not treat a lease renewal or roommate substitution as automatic vacancy decontrol.Rent increase guide ↗ · Roommates: §6.14 / Costa-Hawkins ↗ · Civil Code §827 ↗
02 · Report to the BoardReport required unit information through the Rent Board Portal. Annual deadline: March 1. Reporting is free; late reporting is permitted. Inventory reporting is distinct from the Rent Board fee.Housing Inventory & license ↗
03 · Confirm current licenseReporting generates a license for non-owner-occupied units. Confirm it for each unit and retain evidence covering the effective date. Small ownership does not remove this requirement.Housing Inventory & license ↗
04 · Check interval and effective-date rateFirst annual increase: at least 12 months after lease commencement. Thereafter at least 12 months after the last annual increase. A delayed increase changes the anniversary date. Use the rate on the effective date, not the service date.Rent increase guide ↗
05 · Calculate and explainApply the allowable rate to lawful base rent including housing services, excluding temporary passthroughs/fluctuating charges. Do not round up to a whole dollar. Separately identify annual and banked portions; include banked dates if used.Rent increase guide ↗ · Rent Board Rules, Part IV ↗
06 · Prepare written noticeState old base rent, dollar increase, percentage, new rent and effective date; distinguish annual/banked/certified components. Old/new rent are recommended clarity items. Do not describe an ordinary annual increase as a special approved cost reimbursement.Rent increase guide ↗ · Rent Board Rules, Part IV ↗
07 · Serve correctlyCivil Code §827 permits personal delivery or mail for the periodic tenancies it covers. At most 10% cumulative over the preceding 12 months: at least 30 days. More than 10%: at least 90 days. SF guide says add 5 days for mail; §827 incorporates CCP §1013, so check applicable mailing circumstances. Email alone is not the listed procedure.Civil Code §827 ↗ · Rent increase guide ↗
08 · Retain review packet — recommendationKeep lease/history, license evidence, rate source, calculation, notice and dated proof of service. Check repair complaints, accommodation requests and retaliation issues before proceeding. A rate-compliant increase can still face legal objections.Housing Inventory & license ↗ · Civil Code §1942.5 ↗ · California CRD housing rights ↗
09 · Recheck before collectingConfirm valid license and all timing/lease conditions on the effective date. A license is not substantive approval of the amount. Do not backdate or collect skipped months as retroactive annual rent.Housing Inventory & license ↗ · Rent increase guide ↗ · Banked increases ↗
