First question: do you have to inform the Rent Board or apply for a licence?

The licence: yes. An application: no. Doing this as an individual rather than a corporation changes nothing.

Section 37.15(a) requires every owner of a covered unit to report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory. Once a unit is reported as tenant-occupied, a rent increase licence is generated automatically - there is no form to file for it and no approval to wait for. S1 S2

The licence has to be on file on the effective date of the increase. If it is not, the licence is suspended, the notice is not enforceable, and the tenant owes no increased rent for the months of suspension; reporting later restores it prospectively only. S2

No petition and no Board approval is needed for an ordinary annual or banked increase - the Rent Board's own rate sheet says it may be imposed without filing a petition. S4 S3

Do these three things, in this order

  1. Report both units in the Housing Inventory. Free, online through the Rent Board Portal, due 1 March each year. Best-estimate answers are allowed and late filing carries no monetary penalty, but the licence only exists once the report is in. S1 S2 S12
  2. Confirm the licence shows in the Portal before you serve anything. The licence must be current on the effective date, not on the day you write the notice. S1 S2
  3. Choose the effective date, then serve the right notice. Unit 1 reaches its next anniversary on 1 February 2027; Unit 2 has had no increase since 2019. Use the rate in effect on the effective date, give 30 days, or 90 days if the increase is more than 10 percent, and itemise the annual and banked parts. S5 S8 S7

Your questions, answered in one line

Do I need to apply for a licence?

No application exists. Reporting the unit generates it, and being an individual owner changes nothing.

How much can I raise each unit?

Unit 1: 1.6% now, or 3.0% from 1 Feb 2027 with the skipped year. Unit 2: 1.6% now, or 13.1% with the banked years.

Should I raise one unit or both?

No rule requires both. What gets reviewed is the reason for any difference, so write the reason down per unit.

What is a reasonable or normal increase?

There is no official definition. For a covered unit the lawful ceiling is the published percentage plus any banked amount.

How do I tell the tenants?

Written notice stating the amount, the percentage, the effective date and the banked part: 30 days, or 90 above 10%.
