San Francisco annual rate1.6%: March 1, 2026–February 28, 2027. 1.4%: March 1, 2025–February 28, 2026. 1.7%: March 1, 2024–February 28, 2025. No separate 94122 allowance is established by these citywide sources.Current official rates ↗
Formula is not an extra allowanceSF annual allowance is based on 60% of Bay Area CPI change, with a 7% annual ceiling in §37.3(a)(1). The published 1.6% is the applicable ordinary annual rate for the stated window, not 7%.Rent Ordinance §37.3 ↗ · Current official rates ↗
California capCivil Code §1947.12 generally uses the lower of 5% plus cost-of-living change or 10%, subject to exemptions. Subdivision (d)(3) excludes housing subject to a lower valid local cap. Do not add the state allowance to SF’s allowance.Civil Code §1947.12 ↗
Federal protectionFair housing law prohibits discrimination in rental terms on protected grounds. The cited HUD enforcement source does not establish a general annual rent percentage for ordinary private SF rentals. Subsidized housing needs separate program review.HUD fair housing enforcement ↗ · Civil Code §1947.12 ↗
Banking is not assumedSkipped increases generally require 24 full months without an increase to bank; partial unused portions have separate rules. Banked percentages are added, not compounded or prorated; dates and full history matter. No historical total is calculated here.Banked increases ↗ · Rent Board Rules, Part IV ↗
Annual reasonUse the actual legal basis: “annual allowable increase under the SF Rent Ordinance,” only when eligible. This route does not require inventing repair costs or demonstrating a specific expense increase.Rent Ordinance §37.3 ↗ · Rent Board Rules, Part IV ↗
Special costs / market comparisonsOperating-and-maintenance or capital-improvement increases follow distinct petition/certification rules. Higher expenses or comparable asking rents alone do not authorize an arbitrary increase for a covered tenancy.Rent Ordinance §37.3 ↗
Corporations versus individualsThe cited SF annual-rate and licensing rules govern landlords, without a general individual-owner carveout. State exemptions can depend on ownership form and other conditions; those are not blanket exemptions from SF law.Housing Inventory & license ↗ · Civil Code §1947.12 ↗
Actual industry frequencyUNVERIFIED Official sources reviewed do not establish how often large corporations versus small SF owners actually raise rents. No industry-average percentage or behavior is asserted.Rent increase guide ↗
Practical workflow — recommendation, not surveyBoth owner types can use the same checklist: calendar, verified ledger, license confirmation, standardized calculations, compliant service and consistent documented criteria. A manager or software does not replace compliance.Rent increase guide ↗ · Housing Inventory & license ↗ · Civil Code §827 ↗
