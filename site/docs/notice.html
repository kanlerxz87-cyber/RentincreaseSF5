Do you need a reason?

For the annual allowable increase: no. Nothing in the Rent Ordinance requires a landlord to justify, explain or document a reason for taking the annual increase. It is simply available once every 12 months. That is the whole point of the percentage.

What does require a stated, provable justification is anything above it:

MechanismWhat you must showSource
Banked increaseWhich anniversaries were skipped and the published rate for each. No Rent Board approval needed, but the maths must be right. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Capital improvement passthroughProof of cost and that the work prolongs the life of the building or adds substantially to its value. Requires a petition.San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Operating & maintenance increaseThat your operating expenses rose faster than the annual allowable increase.San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Utility passthroughA completed Rent Board worksheet filed before the notice is served.San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Bond passthroughCompleted worksheet; no petition. San Francisco Rent Board ↗

Put plainly: claiming “my costs went up” is not a basis for exceeding 1.6%. The ordinance has a specific petition for that, and it has to be approved first.

Notice checklist

  • ☐ Housing Inventory report submitted and license current (check the portal)
  • ☐ Rent Board fee paid, or a current exemption on file
  • ☐ At least 12 months since the last increase or since the lease began
  • ☐ Effective date is the anniversary date (or you accept the new anniversary)
  • ☐ Rate used is the one in effect on the effective date
  • ☐ Calculated on base rent only; not rounded up
  • ☐ Banked amounts added, not compounded; each one dated
  • ☐ Dollar amount, percentage and effective date stated
  • ☐ 30 or 90 days counted correctly; +5 days if mailed
  • ☐ Same method applied to every unit
  • ☐ Proof of service retained

Timing trap for Unit B If you serve Unit B a 90-day notice you must already know the percentage that will be in effect on the effective date. The Rent Board publishes around January 1, so a June 1, 2027 effective date is fine — but do not serve a 90-day notice in December 2026 for a March 2027 date and assume last year's rate still applies.
