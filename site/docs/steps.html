Step-by-step checklist

The exact order of operations, with the two notice deadlines already computed for your units.

Do this, in this order

  1. Confirm the property is actually rent-controlled. Built 1940 clears the June 13, 1979 test, but it is not sufficient on its own. If either unit is a single-family home or a condominium, Costa-Hawkins removes the rent-increase ceiling because both tenancies began after January 1, 1996. A single-family dwelling with a legal or illegal in-law unit is a two-unit building and is not exempt. Check the SF Property Information Map. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  2. Pull the full rent history for each unit. Every banked-increase figure depends on it. If any increase was served and you cannot prove it, assume it did not happen. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  3. Report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory at portal.sfrb.org. Required annually by March 1 (next: March 1, 2027). Submitted under penalty of perjury. You will report square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy date, and base rent in $250 increments. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  4. Pay the Rent Board fee. $59.00 per dwelling unit for tax year 2026–27, due March 1. Separate from the license. If the property may be exempt, the 2027 exemption request deadline is Monday, December 14, 2026 — and you must re-request every year. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  5. Confirm the license issued. It is generated automatically for non-owner-occupied units after reporting. It does not get attached to the tenant's notice, but it must be current on the notice's effective date. Tenants can look it up in the portal too. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  6. Pick the effective date — use the anniversary date. Unit A: February 1. Unit B: June 1. Moving the date earlier creates a new anniversary and can permanently forfeit an increase that had not yet been bankable for a full 12 months. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  7. Calculate on base rent only, and do not round up. Base rent includes housing services such as parking or storage. It excludes temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. Banked increases are added, not compounded. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  8. Serve written notice with the required content. Dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and — if any part is banked — which portion is banked and the dates it is based on. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  9. Count the days correctly. 30 days if the increase is 10% or less; 90 days if greater than 10%, alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months. Add 5 days if you mail it. California Legislative Information ↗
  10. Keep proof of service. The Rent Board cannot adjudicate whether notice was received; if it is later disputed, the burden is on you.
