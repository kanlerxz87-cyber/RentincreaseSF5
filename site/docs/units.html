Banking: the one rule that changes everything for Unit B

QuestionOfficial answerSource
How long before a skipped increase can be banked? A full 12 months — Rule 4.12(a): “A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable.” San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Is there a limit on how much can be banked? No. “There is no limit on how much can be banked” (back to April 1, 1982).San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Do banked increases expire? No — not during a tenancy. But they do not carry over to a new tenant.San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Can I compound them? No. “Banked increases shall not be compounded and shall not be rounded up.” Add the percentages, then apply the total to the current base rent.San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Can I take only part of the banked amount? Yes. “If only part of the allowable increase is used, the rest is banked. The remaining portion may be imposed at a later time, after at least 12 months have passed.”San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Does a new owner inherit them? Yes — “New owners may use banked increases from prior owners.” San Francisco Rent Board ↗

Unit A — the replacement occupant matters

Unit A's original lease had three occupants; today it has one of the originals plus a replacement occupant. Three rules govern that, and they point in different directions:

  • You cannot charge more because someone new moved in. “A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge.” San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  • Costa-Hawkins does not help you here. The market-rate reset under Civil Code §1954.53(d) applies to subtenants and assignees who moved in after January 1, 1996. Per the Rent Board, “any co-tenant who moved in after the original occupant is not subject to such a rent increase, regardless of when the co-tenant moved in.” San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  • But Rules & Regulations §6.14 might, later. §6.14 allows an unlimited increase on remaining occupants when the last original occupant vacates — but only if you served each subsequent occupant a written “6.14 notice” within a reasonable time (generally 60 days) of learning about the occupancy. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
Action item — free, and time-sensitive An original occupant is still in Unit A, so no §6.14 or Costa-Hawkins reset is available today. But if you have not already served a written 6.14 notice on the replacement occupant, serve one now. If you wait until the last original occupant leaves, it is too late — the option is lost permanently. The Rent Board recommends getting legal advice before relying on §6.14. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ Form 537 (6.14 / Costa-Hawkins petition) ↗
