Owner practices

How corporate and individual owners are treated, whether both units must move together, and what counts as a defensible reason.

Corporations versus individual owners

The rent cap does not distinguish between them. Section 37.3 applies to 'landlords', and section 37.15 applies to 'owners', with no portfolio threshold. S3 S2 No official empirical comparison of corporate and individual-owner practices was confirmed. Recommendation for either owner type: use an annual compliance calendar, preserve a unit-specific rent ledger, and document calculations and service. This is a suggested workflow, not a verified description of industry practice. S20

One tool commonly associated with large operators is specifically unlawful here: section 37.10C prohibits using algorithmic revenue-management devices that draw on non-public competitor data to set rents, with each month and each unit a separate violation and a private right of action for tenants and tenant nonprofits. S16

One unit or both

Raising one unit and not the other is not itself discrimination. No provision requiring uniform or simultaneous increases was found. S3 The risk is not difference, it is the reason for the difference. A difference explained by tenancy start date, anniversary date and unused banked increases is a business explanation. A difference that tracks a protected characteristic S18 S19 or follows a repair request or a tenant complaint S17 S15 is not.

Raising both units purely to look even is the weaker strategy: it forces an increase on a tenancy that may not need one, and it does not answer the question a reviewer would actually ask. Write down the per-unit reasoning instead.
