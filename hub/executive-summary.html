Executive summary

Compiled 7 November 2026; every figure re-verified against the same official sources on 8 November 2026. The licence question comes first because nothing else can be done safely until it is settled.

1. The licence question

You must be licensed, but you do not apply. Report each unit into the Rent Board Housing Inventory; a rent increase licence is generated automatically for a tenant-occupied unit. It must be current on the effective date of the increase. Individual owners are covered exactly as corporations are. S1 S2

No petition or prior approval is needed for an ordinary annual or properly calculated banked increase. Reporting is required before the effective date, not approval of the proposed notice. Recommended workflow: confirm the licence before serving. A licence is not immunity from a tenant challenge. S3 S13

2. The pending 10% cap on banked increases

Found on 10 November 2026, after the rest of this research was written. The Rent Board posted a notice on 9 November 2026 that the Board of Supervisors is considering File No. 261020, which would cap banked and capital improvement increases at 10% a year and would apply to banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026. It has not been enacted. S5 S6 S24

It does not touch Unit 1 (banked component 1.4%). It is decisive for Unit 2: the full-bank option is 11.5% banked plus 1.6% annual, and the banked part alone exceeds a 10% ceiling. Holding Unit 2 to 10% is lawful today and would stay lawful if the ordinance passes as drafted. S25 See the proposed text and the analysis →

3. What is a normal increase here

For a rent-controlled unit there is no market test. The lawful annual increase is the norm: 1.6% for effective dates from 1 March 2026 through 28 February 2027, once every 12 months, on base rent only, never rounded up. S4 S5 The statewide 8.8% figure does not replace a stricter applicable local cap. Coverage of these particular units remains unverified. No official ZIP-level customary increase was established, and no federal statute caps rent in private, unsubsidised housing in the sources reviewed, so none is estimated here. S9 S10 S19

4. The banking conflict, resolved for these units

The Rent Board's banking page says a skipped increase banks only after 24 full months; Rule 4.12 says 12 months from the date the increase could have been imposed. S6 S7 The Board's own worked example shows the clock runs from the date each increase became available. S5 Because the first increase becomes available 12 months after the tenancy starts, the two wordings describe the same point in time measured from different starting points.

5. One unit or both

No provision requiring identical or simultaneous increases across units was found in the sources reviewed. S3 What the law prohibits is a difference driven by a protected characteristic S18 S19 or a retaliatory or coercive purpose. S15 S17 The two tenancies are in materially different positions - one began in 2025, the other in 2019 with years of unused increases - so treating them differently is explained by the tenancy history, not by the tenants. 6. What to do next

  1. Confirm the Inventory and licence

    Check the public Rent Board Portal for both units. If either is unreported, report it now - it is free and the licence is generated on submission. S1

  2. Confirm coverage

    Verify the 1940 construction date on the Property Information Map. Coverage follows the pre-14 June 1979 rule. S11

  3. Fix the effective dates

    Unit 1: 1 February 2027 keeps the anniversary. Unit 2: on or before 28 February 2027 keeps the published 1.6% annual rate. S4 S5

  4. Choose the size

    Decide between the low-scrutiny annual-only option and the full banked amount. Unit 2 above 10% needs 90 days. S8

  5. Write the file note

    Record the tenancy history, the arithmetic and the business reason. This is what a tenant-side review will ask for. S20

  6. Serve and keep proof

    Serve personally or by mail with the extra 5 days, and keep the proof of service and a copy of the notice. S8 S5
