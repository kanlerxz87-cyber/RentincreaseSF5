The license question, answered first

Question: I am an individual property owner, not a corporation. Do I still have to report to the Rent Board and hold a rent-increase license before notifying tenants of an increase?

Answer: yes on reporting, and the license comes from reporting. San Francisco Administrative Code Sec. 37.15 applies to owners of covered residential units generally - it does not exempt individuals (one of several co-owners may file for all). There is no separate license application form or license fee: reporting a tenant-occupied unit into the Housing Inventory through the Rent Board Portal generates the unit’s rent-increase license on substantial compliance. The practical rule is timing: the license must not be suspended on the increase’s effective date. If reporting lapses, the license is suspended during noncompliance, and even after it is restored the tenant does not owe the increase for the suspended months.

The cited rules do not require attaching the license to the tenant notice, and the City does not state that the license must precede service of the notice - but verifying it before service is the safer workflow. A license also is not approval of the amount and does not prevent a challenge for other violations (amount, timing, service, motive).
