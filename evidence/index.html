The Rent Board process, step by step (official text)

  1. Confirm the units are covered. Many units built on or before 1979-06-13 are rent controlled; a single-family home with a legal in-law unit counts as a two-unit building and is not exempt. The building facts here are owner-reported, so coverage is assumed, not confirmed. Evidence: OCC-01 (official); OCC-02 (official); OWN-04 (official)
  2. Report each tenant-occupied unit into the Rent Board Housing Inventory. Owners of every kind must report (free, online, yearly by March 1). The report is public and made under penalty of perjury. There is no separate license application: the license is generated from the report. Evidence: LIC-02 (official); LIC-03 (official); LIC-08 (official); LIC-13 (official); LIC-14 (official); LIC-16 (official)
  3. Check that a current license shows for each unit and keep a dated screenshot. Status is searchable by address or APN in the portal. Whether these two units have one is not confirmed. Evidence: LIC-15 (official); LIC-18 (official)
  4. Compute the increase from base rent only. Annual 1.6% for 3/1/2026 to 2/28/2027 needs no petition. Banked amounts count only after a full 12 months from the date each became available; no compounding, no rounding up. Evidence: INC-01 (official); INC-02 (official); INC-04 (official); INC-07 (official); INC-10 (official)
  5. Write the notice. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and itemize annual vs banked. A nonconforming notice is null and void. Use the rate in effect on the effective date. Evidence: NTC-04 (official); NTC-05 (official); NTC-06 (official); NTC-09 (official); INC-12 (official)
  6. Serve it on time. At least 30 days before the effective date, or 90 if the increase (with any others in the prior 12 months) exceeds 10%; add 5 days if mailed. Personal delivery or CCP 1013 mail. Evidence: NTC-01 (official); NTC-02 (official); NTC-03 (official)
  7. Screen for retaliation and discrimination before serving. No increase within 180 days of a tenant's good-faith habitability complaint; no differences tied to a protected characteristic; no increase solely because an occupant was added. Evidence: LAW-01 (official); LAW-02 (official); LAW-04 (official); LAW-07 (official); LAW-08 (official); NTC-07 (official); NTC-08 (official)
  8. On the effective date the license must be current. It does not have to be attached to the notice. An increase that takes effect without one may be null and void and the tenant may be entitled to a refund. The Rent Board fee ($59 per unit; 50% recoverable) and City business registration are separate. Evidence: LIC-05 (official); LIC-06 (official); LIC-10 (official); FEE-01 (official); FEE-03 (official); REG-01 (official)
  9. Before serving, re-check the pending cap and the emergency question. File 261020 is pending; an emergency price-gouging rule may or may not apply. Both are flagged below. Evidence: PND-01 (official); PND-02 (official); PND-03 (official); LAW-11 (official); LAW-12 (official)

Analysis (labelled as analysis, not as verified fact)

One unit or both. Different amounts for the two units follow from different lease dates and banking histories: neutral, documented criteria. What the official texts prohibit is differing treatment because of a protected characteristic (LAW-01, LAW-02, LAW-04) and retaliation (LAW-07, LAW-08). No official source reviewed requires equal increases. The record worth keeping is one written method, applied the same way to each unit.

Where a challenge would most likely land. A tenant advocate would check, in order: coverage; a license on the effective date; the arithmetic and the annual/banked itemization; the notice period and service; retaliation timing; and the pending cap. Those are the items the register above lets you evidence.
