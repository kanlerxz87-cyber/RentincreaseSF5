Legal Scrutiny & Tenant Counsel

The premise: every increase should survive review by SF tenant-side advocates and free tenant attorneys. This page maps who can review, what they will test, and the “lawyer-review-ready” checklist.

What this means in practice A rent increase in SF is effectively auditable by a free, city-funded lawyer if any dispute goes to court, and by the Rent Board itself on petition. Designing for that audience — license on file, correct math, clean notice, consistent treatment, complete records — is the cheapest insurance available.

One unit vs. both units — raising one tenant’s rent instead of both

Answer: it is lawful to raise one unit without raising the other — and there is no law requiring both to be raised together or by the same amount. Each tenancy is an independent legal relationship with its own base rent, anniversary date, and banked history. The risk is not in “raising only one”; it is in the difference being caused by a protected characteristic or retaliation:
  • Discrimination: 42 U.S.C. §3604(b) makes it unlawful to discriminate in the terms of rental because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin, or disability — a materially different increase offered on different terms to similar tenants could be examined under this standard if the difference tracks a protected class. (official text)
  • Retaliation: §37.9(d) forbids a rent increase whose dominant motive is retaliation for the tenant exercising rights; a sharply different treatment of the tenant who complained, versus the one who didn’t, is the classic fact pattern. (official text)
  • What would read as “normal business practice” to a tenant lawyer: apply the same mechanic to both units — e.g., “1.6% (2026-27) for both, effective on each unit’s anniversary; banked years taken only where documented.” If the amounts then differ, the difference comes from objective, documented inputs (different anniversary dates, different banked histories, different base rents) — not from who the tenants are.
  • Practical recommendation: for 2026, increase both units by the 1.6% annual rate on their respective anniversary dates (Unit 2 no earlier than 2026-06-01; Unit 1 per its confirmed anniversary). Decide on banked-year treatment before serving notices, apply it identically to both units, and document the decision. This is the posture most likely to be found reasonable, normal, and free of discrimination or legal violation in a tenant-side review.
  • Keep the paper trail neutral: no written statements about tenants’ identities; the notice language identical in substance for both units; the computation file per unit showing the same criteria.

If a review still finds a problem — exposure (verified scope only)

  • Tenants may petition the Rent Board (e.g., unlawful rent increase); the Board conducts hearings/mediations and investigates wrongful evictions. (About Rent Board)
  • Retaliation is a defense to any action to recover possession. (§37.9(d) — official text)
  • Wrongful possession/service-related violations under §37.9(f) expose the landlord to civil damages of not less than three times actual amounts plus costs — cited here for completeness; it attaches to wrongful possession/service conduct, not to a lawful increase. (official text)
  • §37.9F circumvention: civil penalties up to 2× the rent paid/received during the unlawful period, each unit a separate violation. (text)
  • Unlicensed annual/banked increases are not enforceable — the increase simply does not stand. (sf.gov Housing Inventory)
