P01Does an individual (non-corporate) owner need a rent increase license?YES - entity type is irrelevantThe license requirement attaches to the OWNER of a rent-controlled unit, not to a corporation. Sec. 37.15(a) says 'Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board'. Sec. 37.2(h) defines 'Landlord' as an owner/lessor/sublessor and their agent, representative or successor, with no corporate carve-out. A 1-9 unit building has been covered since March 1, 2023.VerifiedS07S14S05S09
P02Is the license a separate application the owner files with the Rent Board?NO - it is generated automaticallyThe official Housing Inventory page states: 'once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.' Reporting is the act that 'informs' the Rent Board; there is no separate license application for annual/banked increases.VerifiedS05S07
P03Does the owner have to notify the Rent Board about the specific rent increase?NO - for annual/banked increasesForm 571 states an owner may increase base rent once every 12 months by the allowable amount 'without filing a petition at the Rent Board'. The sf.gov multi-year page confirms 'Landlords are not required to file a petition with the City to impose these types of rent increases'. The license 'does not need to be included with your rent increase notice'. (A petition IS required for the six listed exception categories - see the Requirements page.)VerifiedS03S11S05S17
P04What must be on file with the Rent Board, and when?A CURRENT Rent Increase License, in effect on the increase's EFFECTIVE DATEsf.gov: 'A current Rent Increase License must be on file with the San Francisco Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect.' Rule 13.14: the effective date, NOT the date of service, is used to decide compliance. So the license must exist before the increase takes effect - not merely before the tenant is notified.VerifiedS06S08S05
P05Consequence of an increase taking effect while unlicensedEntire rent increase null and voidRule 13.14: 'Any rent increase under Ordinance Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2) purporting to be effective while the landlord is unlicensed shall render the entire rent increase null and void.' Sec. 37.15(e): 'a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.' Overpayments may be recoverable by the tenant. Banking rights are preserved.VerifiedS08S07S06
P06Does the license prevent a tenant from challenging the increase?NOA license is permission to impose an annual/banked increase, not approval of the amount. Tenants may file a Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase with NO TIME LIMIT and no filing fee, or a Tenant Summary Petition when a notice is clearly unlawful on its face. sf.gov also warns tenants that a license can be granted even where other problems exist.VerifiedS22S23S06S05

Note: an official Rent Board form (538) states different dates (7/1/2020 and 3/1/2021) - flagged for review, but the ordinance and Rule 13.14 control here.VerifiedS07S08S09S05S43

The underlying statute, in brief

Section 37.15 requires owners to report unit information under penalty of perjury, sets the 7/1/2022 and 3/1/2023 phase-ins, and states that a substantially compliant landlord “shall receive a license to impose rent increases … under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2),” suspended during noncompliance. S07 Rule 13.14 makes an increase effective while unlicensed null and void and fixes the effective date as the test. S08 Form 571 confirms that annual increases need no petition. S03
