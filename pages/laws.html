Local, state and federal rules

Which cap actually governs, why the statewide cap does not apply here, and what federal law does — and does not — say about rent increases.

In one line

These units are governed by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance alone. The statewide cap (AB 1482) expressly does not apply to housing already limited by a local ordinance below the state figure S14 S15, and there is no federal rent cap at all. Federal law enters through fair housing, which is about why an increase is imposed, not how much it is S25 S26.

State detail

AB 1482 limits increases to 5% + CPI or 10%, whichever is lower, and to no more than two increments in 12 months S15. For San Francisco the Rent Board publishes 8.8% for 1 August 2026 to 31 July 2027 — and states on the same page that it does not apply to units under the Rent Ordinance increase limits S14. The statutory basis is §1947.12(d)(3), which excludes housing subject to a local ordinance restricting annual increases below the state figure S15. At 1.6% these units are below 8.8%, so the local cap controls.

AB 1482 also contains a detail worth knowing: the exemption for single-family homes and condominiums is unavailable to an owner that is a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member S15 S14. It is irrelevant to a two-unit building, but it explains why corporate ownership is not a privileged position in California rent law.

Costa-Hawkins lets an owner set the initial rate for a vacant unit S17, which is why a new tenancy can begin at a market rent; the Rent Board says the same in plain terms S1 S24. It is also why a two-unit building stays covered: only units alienable separately from the title to another dwelling — single-family homes and condominiums — fall outside local rent limits, and a house with an in-law unit counts as two units S13.

Notice mechanics. 30 days for an increase of 10% or less, 90 days for more than 10%, either in itself or combined with other increases in the preceding 12 months; personal delivery, or mail under Code of Civil Procedure §1013 S16. The Rent Board restates this and adds five days where the notice is mailed S1.

Federal detail

There is no federal statute capping residential rent. The federal role is fair housing enforcement: discrimination in the terms and conditions of a rental — which includes how rent is set — can be challenged through HUD's Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity office S26 and, at state level, through the California Civil Rights Department under FEHA S25. FEHA's protected list includes race, colour, ancestry, national origin, citizenship, immigration status, primary language, religion, disability, sex, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, familial status, source of income (including a Section 8 voucher), military status and age S25. Retaliation for asserting rights is separately prohibited S25.

A HUD Fair Market Rent benchmark for a two-bedroom unit in this area would be useful context, but no official FY2026/27 figure could be confirmed this session: the HUD summary page rendered without values, and secondary sources disagree ($2,850 / $3,174 / $3,604 / $3,697). It is therefore left blank and logged as F5 rather than estimated.
