Allowable annual increases

The complete official table, matching the Rent Board's own rate sheet, plus how the annual figure is set and how it interacts with banked years.

How the annual figure is set

Each 1 March the Rent Board publishes the increase in the CPI for the preceding 12 months. A landlord who has reported the unit to the Rent Board “shall have a licence to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase”, and in no event more than 7% S5. The current allowance is 1.6% for 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027 S1 S2.

Three mechanical rules matter as much as the percentage: an increase may be given only once in any 12 months S1; it is calculated on the base rent and cannot be rounded up S1; and skipped years are added, not compounded, and cannot be rounded up S9.

Banking, in one paragraph

A landlord who refrains from imposing an annual increase, or any part of it, may accumulate the amount and impose it on or after a later anniversary date; banked amounts are given only at the time of an annual increase, only once every 12 months, are not compounded and are not rounded up, and a full 12 months must have elapsed from the date the increase could first have been imposed S9 S10. Banking has been permitted only for increases available on or after 1 April 1982, there is no cap on the total banked amount under the current rules, banked amounts do not carry over to a new tenant, and a new owner inherits the bank S11.

Two official pages disagree about the waiting period. The Rent Board's “Banked rent increases” page says a skipped increase is banked only if “at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase” S11, while Rule 4.12 and the “Learn about rent increases” page both say a full 12 months must elapse S9 S1. We did not choose between them. The conservative assumption — 24 months — is the one flagged on Units & numbers, and the discrepancy is item F1 on Flagged for review.
