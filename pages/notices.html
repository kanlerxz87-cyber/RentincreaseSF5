Notices

The notice is where most increases fail. What it must contain, how far ahead it must be served, and the defects that void it.

The eight defects that void an increase

Each of these is a reason a tenant petition succeeds
DefectWhy it failsSource
Percentage above the allowance without a petitionNull and void — the Rules void the entire increase, not only the excess; only a certified petition authorises more.S5 S19 S9

The ordinance's own words are blunt: “Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void” S5, and Rule 4.10(b) says a non-conforming increase “shall render the entire rent increase null and void” S9. The Rules then carve out a narrow good-faith adjustment: where an increase given in a good-faith effort to comply with the ordinance and rules does not exceed the limitations by more than one-half of one percent of the prior base rent, an Administrative Law Judge readjusts the base rent to the proper percentage instead of voiding it; Rule 4.12 contains a parallel provision for banked amounts S9. That is a drafting-error margin, not a safe harbour — do not plan around it.

Notice-preparation checklist

A checklist, not a legal template. Have counsel review the final wording, and read it back to the Rent Board counsellor before service.

  1. Unit and tenant names exactly as they appear on the lease (placeholders in this record).
  2. Current base rent and the new base rent, in dollars.
  3. The dollar increase and the percentage, to one decimal place.
  4. The composition — for example “1.4% banked (available 1 February 2026) + 1.6% annual = 3.0%” for Unit A, or “11.5% banked (2020–2025) + 1.6% annual = 13.1%” for Unit B.
  5. The effective date, chosen so the lead time is at least 30 or 90 days, plus five if mailed.
  6. The date of the last increase, or the tenancy start date if there has been none.
  7. A statement that the increase is calculated under the Rent Ordinance's annual allowance and banked allowances, with the yearly percentages itemised.
  8. A dated internal record that the Housing Inventory report is filed and the rent increase licence is live.
  9. Proof of service: method, date and recipient.
  10. A copy filed with the rent ledger, and the new figure reported in the next March 1 Housing Inventory update.

Worked example of the composition sentence

Unit B, effective 1 June 2027: “Your monthly rent will increase by $412.65, from $3,150.00 to $3,562.65 (13.1%). This increase is composed of banked annual allowances of 11.5% (1.8% available 1 June 2020; 0.7% 2021; 2.3% 2022; 3.6% 2023; 1.7% 2024; 1.4% 2025) and the annual allowance of 1.6% in effect on 1 June 2027. Banked allowances are added and not compounded.”

Every element of that sentence answers a specific requirement: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, annual versus banked split, and the years in the bank S1 S5 S9 S11. Leave a blank for the 2027-28 annual rate if the notice is finalised after the Rent Board publishes it.
