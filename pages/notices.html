How to Notify Tenants of a Rent Increase

Timing · Content · Delivery · What tenants may do in response

Timing — written notice required

Increase sizeMinimum noticeIf mailedSource
10% or less (alone or combined with another increase in the same 12 months)30 days before the effective date+5 days (subject to Civ. Code Sec. 1013)CA Civil Code Sec. 827(b)(2) · SF — Learn about rent increases
More than 10% (alone or combined)90 days before the effective date+5 days (subject to Civ. Code Sec. 1013)CA Civil Code Sec. 827(b)(3) · SF — Learn about rent increases

The 30/90-day rule applies to residential dwellings (Civ. Code Sec. 827(b)). The Rent Board's guidance matches: 30-day written notice; 90-day written notice if the increase exceeds 10%.

What tenants can do — and why procedure matters

San Francisco tenants have strong, free channels to challenge a rent increase. Any increase should be able to pass review by a tenant advocate or lawyer; that means being procedurally perfect: current license, correct notice period, correct content, correct calculation.

Practical sequence: (1) confirm coverage and anniversary dates; (2) report into the Housing Inventory and obtain the rent increase license; (3) calculate the increase (annual + banked, or petition-approved); (4) serve a correct written notice (30 or 90 days, correct content); (5) keep records.
