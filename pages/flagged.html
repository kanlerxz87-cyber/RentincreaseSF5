F1  official sources conflict

Two official Rent Board pages give different banking waiting periods

The “Banked rent increases” page says a skipped increase is banked only if “at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase” S11; Rule 4.12 and the “Learn about rent increases” page say a full 12 months must elapse S9 S1.

What it affects: Unit A's 2026-02-01 allowance and any future skipped year.

Recommended action: Ask the Rent Board counsellor which is current, and whether the 1/13/26 rule amendments changed Rule 4.12. Until then, treat the earlier year as at risk if the anniversary date is moved.

Sources: S11 S9 S1 S10

F2  pending legislation

File 261020 (10% cap on banked and capital-improvement increases) — pending, not enacted

Board of Supervisors File 261020, introduced 15 September 2026, would cap banked plus capital-improvement increases at 10% per year for three years. The official Legistar status page reads Pending Committee Action. The Rent Board posted an official notice about it on 9 November 2026.

What it affects: Directly changes Unit B's 13.1% to 10% this cycle if enacted as described.

Recommended action: Check the Board of Supervisors legislative record and the Rent Board's news page before drafting any Unit B notice; if enacted, recompute and carry the balance forward.

Sources: S18 S28

F3  unverified

Unit A base rent of $3,995.00 set on 2025-02-01 — lawfulness unverified

A new-tenancy initial rent may be set without limit for an empty unit S1, but the ordinance withdraws that for five years where the prior tenancy ended by an owner notice under Civil Code §1946.1 or a §827 change of terms S5. How the prior tenancy ended was not supplied.

What it affects: If the reset was unavailable, every figure computed from $3,995.00 is wrong.

Recommended action: Obtain the document that ended the prior tenancy (notice, surrender, buyout or vacate letter) and the prior rent history; if it was an owner no-fault notice, recompute from the prior lawful rent.

Sources: S1 S5 S17

F4  unverified

Tenant A2 is a continuing occupant, not an original lessee

Whether a Rules & Regs §6.14 notice or a Costa-Hawkins reset was ever served on Tenant A2 is unknown. That determines whether a new tenancy was created for rent purposes when the last original occupant left S24.

What it affects: Changes both the base rent and the anniversary date for Unit A.

Recommended action: Search the file for a §6.14 notice. If none was served within a reasonable time of learning of the occupancy, obtain advice before treating the base rent as settled.

Sources: S24 S17 S5

F5  unverified

HUD Fair Market Rent for this ZIP area — no official value confirmed

The FY2026/27 HUD FMR summary page rendered without values and the API requires a token. Secondary sites give $2,850, $3,174, $3,604 and $3,697 for a two-bedroom unit.

What it affects: Market context only; it does not change the lawful maximum.

F6  no provision found

Rent Board fee payment is not tied to increase validity in Chapter 37

§37.15(e) ties the licence to Housing Inventory reporting S4. No provision was found in Chapter 37 making an increase void for non-payment of the Chapter 37A fee, although the fee is mandatory and penalties accrue S8.

What it affects: Procedural and tenant-relations risk, not an identified legal bar.

Recommended action: Pay the fee by 1 March and keep the receipt. Recorded as an absence-of-authority finding rather than assumed to be a requirement.

Sources: S4 S7 S8

F7  no provision found

No provision requires the same increase across units

Chapter 37 and the Rules were reviewed for an equal-treatment requirement across tenancies S18 S5. None was found. Discrimination law operates on reason and effect, not on identical percentages S25.

What it affects: Bears on the one-unit-or-both decision.

Recommended action: Adopt the written policy on the Owner playbook page and apply it uniformly; have counsel review the decision if only one unit is increased.

Sources: S18 S5 S25

F8  unverified

Unit A: the 2026-04-01 entry is an assumption, not a fact

The owner's record shows a lease start of 2025-02-01 at $3,995.00, then a “continuing tenancy” dated 2026-04-01 at the same rent for two occupants, one of whom was an original lessee. This site reads that as a partial change of occupants within one continuing tenancy, because a new tenancy for rent purposes requires the last original occupant to vacate or cease permanently residing in the unit S24 S5.

What it affects: If it was in fact a wholly new lease with no original occupant remaining, Unit A's anniversary date is 1 April, not 1 February, and the available allowance and effective date change.

Recommended action: Confirm from the signed documents whether Tenant A1 has been in continuous occupancy since 2025-02-01 and whether a new lease was signed in April 2026. If a new tenancy began, recompute: the first increase would be available 2027-04-01.

Sources: S24 S5 S1
