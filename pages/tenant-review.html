Dashboard / Tenant-lawyer test

Would this pass a tenant-side review?

A tenant can petition at any time, for free, with no deadline. These are the fifteen questions that review will actually ask, and the document that answers each one.

How the review works

A tenant files the Tenant Petition and Form C, checks box C for an unlawful rent increase, pays nothing, and there is no time limit S20. A completed petition is served on the owner and usually heard within a few months by a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge; the decision can be appealed to the Rent Board Commission S20 S18. The frame is not “was this a nice thing to do” but “does the arithmetic and the paperwork conform to the ordinance” — and a non-conforming increase is void S5.

Tenants in San Francisco also have access to free legal help: the Rent Board's own termination notice tells tenants they are eligible for the Tenant Right to Counsel Program through the Eviction Defense Collaborative S27. Assume the file will be read by someone who does this every week.

Where the file is strongest and weakest

Strongest: Unit B's arithmetic. Seven years of published percentages, added, on a base rent that has not moved since 2019. That is the textbook banked increase, and every element is checkable against the Rent Board's own table.
Weakest: Unit B's size. 13.1% in one notice will be petitioned even when it is correct, and it is the target of the pending 10% cap. The defence is documentation, not persuasion.
Weakest: Unit A's base rent. A base rent set at a new tenancy is assumed lawful, but if the prior vacancy followed an owner notice the ordinance limits the new initial rent for five years S5, and if Tenant A2 was never served a §6.14 notice the reset may not be available at all S24. Both are open items (F3 and F4).

What to hold in the file, per unit

  • The lease showing the start date and the initial rent.
  • Every rent increase notice ever served, with proof of service.
  • A year-by-year calculation sheet naming the official percentage for each year S2.
  • The Housing Inventory submission confirmation and the licence status, dated S3.
  • Proof the Rent Board fee was paid, or the exemption S7.
  • Any §6.14 notice served on a subsequent occupant, with the date S24.
  • The written rent-increase policy and the record that it was applied to both units S25.
