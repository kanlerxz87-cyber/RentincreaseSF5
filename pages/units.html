Units and numbers

Anonymised tenancy facts, the year-by-year allowance ledger for each unit, and the arithmetic that follows from it. Owner-supplied facts are labelled as such; nothing is inferred.

Anonymisation

The owner supplied tenant names, initials and a ZIP-level location. Those identifiers are not published: the site uses Tenant A1, Tenant A2, Tenant B1, Tenant B2 and Unit A / Unit B. Dates and dollar amounts are preserved exactly, because the arithmetic depends on them. No street address, parcel number or owner name appears anywhere in this repository.

Interpretation note — how the Unit A dates were read. The owner's record gives a lease start of 2025-02-01 at $3,995.00 for three original lessees, then a “continuing tenancy” entry dated 2026-04-01 at the same $3,995.00 for two current occupants, one of whom (Tenant A1) was an original lessee. This site therefore treats the tenancy that began 2025-02-01 as continuing, with 1 February as the anniversary date, because a new tenancy for rent purposes arises only when the last original occupant vacates or no longer permanently resides in the unit S24 S5, and Tenant A1 has not. If that reading is wrong — for example if a wholly new lease was signed on 2026-04-01 with no original occupant remaining — the anniversary date becomes 1 April and the arithmetic changes. That is item F8 on Flagged for review.

Open question that changes the base rent itself. The $3,995.00 base was set at a new tenancy on 2025-02-01. The Rent Board states there is no limit on what an owner may first charge for an empty unit S1, but the ordinance withdraws that right for five years where the previous tenancy ended by an owner notice under Civil Code §1946.1 or a §827 change of terms S5. If the prior tenancy ended any other way, and the unit was genuinely empty, the base is sound. This is unverified: item F3 on Flagged for review.

Check before drafting: Ordinance 261015. A bill introduced 15–18 September 2026 would cap banked and capital-improvement increases for rent-controlled units at 10% per year for three years.
