Step-by-Step: Lawful Rent Increase (SF, rent-controlled)

Eight steps, each with its official source. The short version: report units → license → compute on base rent → 12-month timing → 30/90-day written notice → proof → documentation file.

  1. Verify coverage of each unit
    SF Admin Code Ch. 37 (enacted 1979) caps increases for most units in buildings with a certificate of occupancy on or before 1979-06-13 (multi-unit; SFH/condos generally outside the rent-increase cap). Confirm the building’s actual CO date in the official Assessor-Recorder search; if the unit were exempt from the local cap, the state AB 1482 cap (5% + CPI, max 10%) and its exemption notices would apply instead.
    verified sf.gov — Learn about SF rental laws · About Rent Board · Assessor-Recorder search (official tool) · state cap: Civ. Code §1947.12
  2. Report each tenant-occupied unit to the Housing Inventory (by March 1 annually)
    Free online reporting through the Rent Board Portal, under penalty of perjury. Required for ALL owners of residential property — “even if you are not a landlord.” Provide: your/agent contact info, business registration number if any, approximate square footage (250 sq ft increments), bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy start date, base rent (in $250 increments) and whether utilities are included. The submission is public.
    verified sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · portal: portal.sfrb.org
  3. Confirm the rent increase license exists and is current
    The license is auto-generated when a tenant-occupied unit is reported. “A landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect.” If the 2026-03-01 deadline was missed: submit now — late reporting is accepted with no monetary penalty, but the increase is unenforceable until the license is in effect on the notice’s effective date. The license does not need to be attached to the notice.
    verified sf.gov — Housing Inventory (“The rent increase license”, “Deadline”)
  4. Pay the annual Rent Board fee (separate from the license; due March 1)
    $59.00 per dwelling unit (2026-27 tax year) via the Portal; 5%/month late penalty (max 15%); delinquent after June 1. If you pay in full you may recover 50% ($29.50/unit) from the tenant (tenant must have occupied as of Nov 1 of the tax year). Penalty fees are not recoverable. Exemption requests (owner-occupied, government-regulated, etc.) are filed October–early December.
    verified sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee
  5. Compute the increase on base rent only
    Total = (annual rate in effect on the increase’s effective date) + (banked rates from skipped years, added). 2026-03-01 → 2027-02-28 rate: 1.6%. Base rent includes housing services (parking/storage) but not temporary passthroughs or fluctuating charges. Rounding up to the nearest dollar is not allowed — compute to the cent. A banked year must have been available for a full 12 months before it can be applied.
    verified sf.gov — 1.6% announcement · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases (worked examples) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
  6. Respect the 12-month anniversary-date rule
    First increase: 12 months after the lease began. After any increase, its effective date is the new anniversary date; no further increase before 12 months. Choosing a different effective date can reset the anniversary and permanently lose an increase that hadn’t completed its 12-month banking window (official worked example).
    verified sf.gov — Learn about rent increases (Timing and anniversary date)
  7. Serve the written notice on time, with the required content
    30 days’ written notice if the increase (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months) is ≤10%; 90 days if >10%; add 5 days if mailed (CCP §1013). Rent Board guidance: the notice should include the dollar amount of the increase, the percentage, and the effective date — using the percentage in effect on the increase date. Deliver personally or by mail (CCP procedures); keep dated proof of service. If any portion is banked, identify the banked portions and their years in the notice.
    verified sf.gov — Giving notice · Civ. Code §827 (official text)
  8. Keep the compliance file review-ready
    For each increase: the computation, the license status on the effective date (Portal printout), the notice, proof and date of service, and the updated rent ledger. Tenants may file an unlawful-rent-increase petition with the Rent Board, and Prop F provides free attorneys in eviction matters — the file is your defense in both venues. Rent Board counseling is free (311 / 415-701-2311).
    verified About Rent Board (services) · Eviction Defense Collaborative (Prop F lead agency) — the documentation step itself is a best-practice recommendation
