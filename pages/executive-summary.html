Executive Summary

Two rent-controlled units in San Francisco (Outer Sunset, 94122), building c. 1940 (owner-stated; coverage to be verified — F-04). Prepared 2026-11-07. Not legal advice.

1. Do I need a license and must I inform the Rent Board — even as an individual owner?

Answer: Yes. Individual owners are fully covered — the rule makes no corporate/individual distinction. In San Francisco, every owner of residential property — including individual owners who are not professional landlords — must report tenant-occupied units into the Rent Board Housing Inventory (annually, by March 1). Once a tenant-occupied unit is reported, the Rent Board automatically generates a “rent increase license.” A landlord must have that license on file before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect; without it the increase is not enforceable, even if the percentage was correct.
  • It is not a discretionary “application for approval.” There is no petition, fee, or approval decision for the license itself — reporting the units (free, under penalty of perjury) is the mechanism. The license does not need to be attached to the rent increase notice. verified — sf.gov: The Rent Board Housing Inventory
  • “Inform the Rent Board” = the annual Housing Inventory report. The Rent Board’s own wording: “Most owners are required to do this even if you are not a landlord.” verified — sf.gov (same page, Overview)
  • Missing it has teeth: “your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice,” and the Rent Board may notify your tenants of the noncompliance. Tenants can check your license status in the public Rent Board Portal. verified — sf.gov (same page, Deadline)
  • When this took effect: July 1, 2022 (buildings with 10+ units); March 1, 2023 (condos and buildings with 1–9 units — the category this 2-unit building falls in). verified — sf.gov and sf.gov: Learn about rent increases
  • Separate obligation — the Rent Board fee: $59.00 per dwelling unit (2026-27 tax year), due March 1; paying in full lets you recover 50% ($29.50) from the tenant. This fee is not the license and does not satisfy inventory reporting. verified — sf.gov: The Rent Board Fee

Why “informing the Rent Board” protects you: a licensed, properly noticed, within-cap increase is the profile that survives a tenant-filed unlawful rent increase petition or a Prop F attorney’s review. See Legal Scrutiny.

2. The process, step by step (each step linked to an official source)

  1. Confirm your units are rent-controlled
    The SF Rent Ordinance (Admin Code Ch. 37, enacted 1979) covers most units in buildings with a certificate of occupancy on or before June 13, 1979 (2+ units; single-family homes/condos generally exempt from the rent increase cap). Your 1940 building (owner-stated) is almost certainly covered — verify the actual CO date via the official Assessor-Recorder search (F-04).
    Sources: sf.gov — Learn about SF rental laws · About Rent Board · Assessor-Recorder property search
  2. Create a Rent Board Portal account and report each tenant-occupied unit (Housing Inventory)
    Report through portal.sfrb.org by March 1 every year (late is allowed — no monetary penalty — but you cannot legally impose an increase without the license on the effective date). Reporting is free and done under penalty of perjury; the data is public (bedrooms, approx. square footage, base rent in $250 increments, occupancy history, your/agent contact info).
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
  3. Confirm the rent increase license is in effect
    The license is automatically generated once a tenant-occupied unit is reported. Check it in the Portal before you date your notice. (Tenants can check it there too — assume they will.)
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory (“The rent increase license”)
  4. Pay the annual Rent Board fee (due March 1)
    $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026-27; 5% late penalty per month (max 15%); 50% ($29.50) may be recovered from the tenant if you pay in full and the tenant occupied the unit on November 1 of the tax year.
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee
  5. Compute the lawful amount (base rent only; never round up)
    Amount = base rent × (annual rate in effect on the effective date + banked rates from skipped years, added not compounded). For 2026-03-01 → 2027-02-28 the annual rate is 1.6%. Worked numbers for your two units: Units & Analysis.
    Sources: sf.gov — 1.6% announcement (2026-01-14) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases (worked examples)
  6. Check timing: 12 months since the last increase (the “anniversary date”)
    First increase is 12 months after the lease began; each increase’s effective date becomes the new anniversary date. No increase is allowed before that date. If you move the date, an increase that hasn’t completed a 12-month availability window can be permanently lost.
    Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases (Timing and anniversary date)
  7. Serve the written notice: 30 days (90 if the increase, alone or combined in 12 months, exceeds 10%; +5 days if mailed)
    The notice should state the dollar amount of the increase, the percentage, and the effective date; use the percentage in effect on the increase date. Keep proof of service (delivery date and method). If part of the increase is banked, the notice should identify the banked portions and their years.
    Sources: sf.gov — Giving notice · Cal. Civ. Code §827 (official text)
  8. Document everything and keep the file review-ready
    Rent ledger (every increase: date, amount, percentage, license in force, notice + proof of service), Housing Inventory confirmations, fee receipts. This file is exactly what a tenant-side attorney or Rent Board petition will test. See the scrutiny checklist.
    Basis: About Rent Board (petitions, investigations, counseling) — documentation requirement is a compliance recommendation, not a quoted rule
Standing constraints (apply at every step) • No increase based on a protected class (race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin, disability — 42 U.S.C. §3604) and no retaliatory increase (§37.9(d) — official text). • No extra rent for an added roommate — unlawful even if the lease says so (sf.gov). • Using a rent increase to circumvent tenant protections is independently actionable (§37.9F — see Rules).

3. What is “reasonable and normal” for your units?

  • The lawful maximum is the benchmark. For increases effective 2026-03-01 → 2027-02-28 the SF cap is 1.6% of base rent (official announcement), plus documented banked increases (officially added, not compounded). An increase at or below that, licensed and properly noticed, is within normal business practice — it is what the law itself authorizes every year.
  • Unit 2 (anniversary June 1; no documented increases since 2019-06-01 — verify, F-02): up to 12.1% lawfully available at 2026-06-01 ($3,150 → $3,531.15, 90-day notice), or just 1.6% now ($3,150 → $3,200.40, 30-day notice) with the rest banked. Full math: Units & Analysis.
  • Unit 1 ($3,995): the exact total depends on the pre-2025-02-01 rent ledger (F-01). If 2025-02-01 was the last increase, an increase effective 2026-03-01 or later may be 1.6% + 1.4% banked = 3.0% ($3,995 → $4,114.85, 30-day notice).
  • How big operators do it (industry practice, non-legal): annual cycle, 60–90 days advance communication, market/cost documentation, gradual 3–5% steps, renewal incentives — Practice page.
  • One unit vs. both: no law requires raising both units together or by the same amount; each tenancy is judged on its own cap/notice/license. The discrimination risk exists only if the difference is tied to a protected class or retaliation. A uniform, modest, documented increase for both units is the lowest-scrutiny path. Details: Legal Scrutiny.

5. Bottom line

  1. Report both units into the Housing Inventory now (the 2026-03-01 deadline has passed — late reporting is still accepted and generates the license), confirm the licenses, and pay/keep the fees current.
  2. Next lawful increase window: Unit 2 — no earlier than 2026-06-01; Unit 1 — no earlier than the anniversary date (2026-02-01 if 2025-02-01 was the last increase; otherwise per the rent ledger).
  3. Use 1.6% (2026-27) + verified banked amounts, base rent only, no rounding up, 30/90-day written notice with proof of service.
  4. Treat both units consistently, keep the documentation file review-ready, and route final figures through a quick attorney/Rent Board counseling check before serving notices (Rent Board counseling is free: 311).
