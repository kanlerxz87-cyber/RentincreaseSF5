Executive summary — the Rent Board license question comes first

The full executive summary: the license answer, step-by-step process, and every other question answered with official links.

Verified 2026-11-07 · UTC

1. The Rent Board “license” — do you need it as an individual owner?

Yes. Section 37.15 applies to “Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37,” and Section 37.2(h) defines “Landlord” as “an owner, lessor, sublessor … and the agent, representative or successor of any of the foregoing.” There is no corporate carve-out. Condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 residential units have been covered since March 1, 2023 (10+ unit buildings since July 1, 2022). S07S14S05S09

But three details in the question need correcting:

  • Not a separate application. “Once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.” S05
  • No notice/petition about this specific increase. Form 571: the allowable annual increase may be imposed “without filing a petition at the Rent Board”; the license “does not need to be included with your rent increase notice.” S03S11S05
  • The effective date, not the notice date, controls. “A current Rent Increase License must be on file … before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect”; Rule 13.14: “the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used.” An increase effective while unlicensed “shall render the entire rent increase null and void.” S06S08S07
  • It does not prevent a challenge. Tenants may file an unlawful-rent-increase petition that is free with no time limit, or a Tenant Summary Petition. S22S23

2. Step by step

  1. Confirm coverage — built on/before June 13, 1979, not single-family; verify on the official Property Information Map. S18S36
  2. Report into the Housing Inventory by March 1 each year — under penalty of perjury; owner/manager contact, unit size, occupancy dates, base rent in $250 increments, utilities. S05S07
  3. Confirm the license is current in the Rent Board Portal for the relevant year. S06S37
  4. Pay the Rent Board fee separately — $59.00 per dwelling unit (2026-27); 50% recoverable from a qualifying tenant. S21
  5. Calculate — rate in effect on the effective date (1.6%), on base rent only, once per 12 months, no compounding, no rounding up. S01S02S04S03
  6. Serve written notice — 30 days (≤10%) or 90 days (>10%), +5 days if mailed; include dollar amount, percentage, effective date and any banked breakdown. S30S01S10S13S15
  7. On the effective date the license must still be current; keep the whole compliance file. S08S22
Six exception categories do require a landlord petition before the notice is served (capital improvements, O&M, comparable rents, Proposition I, utility passthrough, Rule 1.21). S17
