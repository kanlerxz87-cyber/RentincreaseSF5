Do I need the Rent Board license?

The short version: you don't apply for permission, but you must hold a current license — and being an individual owner is not an exemption.

Overview License Checklist Rates Units Notice Flagged Sources
Short answer You do not apply for permission to raise the rent, but you must hold a current rent increase license, which you get automatically by completing the annual Housing Inventory report. This applies to individual owners exactly as it does to corporations.

What the law actually says

San Francisco Administrative Code §37.15(e):

“If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance. Upon receipt of the required information from the landlord, the suspension shall be terminated, and the license to impose rent increases shall be restored prospectively, but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”

San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗

And §37.3(a)(1) makes the license a precondition of the increase itself: the clause reads “A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase…” San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗

What happens if you serve a notice without a license

The increase is unenforceable for the suspended months.

§37.15(e): the license is restored only prospectively, and the tenant “shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”

The Rent Board may tell your tenants.

If you miss the deadline, the Rent Board may notify occupants that you are not in compliance.

No penalty for filing late — but no increase either.

The Rent Board “does not assess monetary penalties for filing after the deadline.” The loss is the increase itself.
