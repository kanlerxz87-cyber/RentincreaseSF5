Corporate vs. individual owners

The rules are the same. Here is the one place the statute actually treats them differently.

Overview License Checklist Rates Units Notice Flagged Sources
The headline San Francisco's rent-increase rules do not distinguish between a large corporate landlord and one person owning a two-unit building. The ordinance says “landlord” and §37.15 says “owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37.” There is no size threshold and no incorporation test anywhere in it.

Where the law actually draws a line

ProvisionEffectSource
Civil Code §1947.12(d)(5)(A)(ii) The AB 1482 exemption for a single-family home or condo held separately from any other dwelling unit is not available to a corporation (nor to a REIT, nor to an LLC in which a corporation holds membership). A natural-person owner can qualify; a corporate owner cannot.California Legislative Information ↗
Rent Ordinance §37.10C No landlord in San Francisco may use “revenue management software” that calculates non-public competitor data to set or recommend rents. Up to $1,000 per violation, per month, per unit, with a tenant right of action and attorney's fees. San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗
Rent Ordinance §37.10B Tenant harassment — bad-faith conduct to influence a tenant to vacate. Unlawful rent increases are a recurring fact pattern in these claims. San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗

Practice differences — not from an official source

Everything in this section is observation, not verified fact. No official source was found that documents how differently-sized owners behave. It is recorded here because you asked, and flagged because it cannot be cited.
ObservationStatus
Institutional owners tend to impose the annual increase every single year on the anniversary date, so little or nothing ever accumulates as banked increases. Unverified
Institutional owners are more likely to file capital improvement and O&M petitions, because the filing is free and they have the records to document costs. Unverified
Smaller individual owners more often skip years, then face a large banked increase later — which is exactly the situation Unit B is in. Unverified

What is verifiable is the mechanism each group uses, and it is the same mechanism: the annual allowable increase, banked increases, and approved petitions. There is no separate “corporate” rent increase in San Francisco law. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
