San Francisco Administrative Code §37.15 requires every owner of a residential dwelling unit to report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory. Only a landlord who has “substantially complied” receives a license to impose rent increases under §§37.3(a)(1)–(2). Executive summary

1. The license question — answered

Do you need to apply for a license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants? Yes to the first part, no to the second — and being an individual rather than a corporation makes no difference.

  • You do not need to file a petition, get advance approval, or send the Rent Board a copy of your notice for an ordinary annual or banked increase. San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗
  • You do need a current rent increase license, which the Rent Board generates automatically once you complete the annual Housing Inventory report for a non-owner-occupied unit. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  • You must also pay the annual Rent Board fee. It is a separate obligation from the license, with its own March 1 deadline and 5%-per-month late penalty. San Francisco Rent Board ↗
  • The statute says “owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37.” There is no corporation / individual distinction anywhere in §37.15, and the fee page states the fee applies to “all residential property owners… This includes single-family homes and condominiums.” San Francisco Rent Ordinance ↗

Consequence of getting it wrong: under §37.15(e) the license is temporarily suspended during noncompliance, it is restored only prospectively, and “a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.” You cannot retroactively collect those months. San Francisco Rent Board ↗

2. What a “reasonable and normal” increase looks like

Both units are in a building built in 1940, so they sit on the covered side of San Francisco's June 13, 1979 cutoff. The starting point for any increase is the single percentage the Rent Board publishes each year: 1.6% for March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027. San Francisco Rent Board ↗ A landlord may impose it once every 12 months, on the tenant's anniversary date, and it requires no justification at all — no cost increase, no improvement, no reason needs to be stated. San Francisco Rent Board ↗

Anything above that has to come from a specific, documented mechanism: banked increases from years you skipped, or a Rent Board–approved petition. San Francisco Rent Board ↗

The other questions, in one line each

Local, state and federal limits

San Francisco's 1.6% is the binding number. The state AB 1482 cap (5% + CPI, max 10%) does not stack on top of it — Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing already under a lower local cap. Federal law adds no rent ceiling but bans discriminatory increases.

Corporations vs. individuals

The rent-increase rules are identical. The one place the statute actually distinguishes them: a corporation can never use the AB 1482 single-family-home / condo exemption (§1947.12(d)(5)(A)(ii)). San Francisco also bans algorithmic rent-setting software for everyone.

How to tell tenants

Written notice with the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and — if any of it is banked — which portion is banked and the dates it is based on. 30 days if the increase is 10% or less, 90 days if more. Add 5 days if you mail it.

Raise one unit or both?

No law requires uniformity, but applying the same method to both units on each unit's own anniversary date is the most defensible posture. What creates exposure is a difference in treatment that lines up with a protected characteristic or follows a tenant complaint.
