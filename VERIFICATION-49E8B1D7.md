## 2. Licence question — verified verbatim, now forefront as requested

> **Owner question:** "I read I need to inform the Rent Board and apply for a license to prevent the rent increase from being challenged. I am just an individual property owner, not a corporation — do I still need to apply for the license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants?"

**Answer in plain language, with the official sentence for each claim:**

1. **Yes, you must inform the Rent Board — every owner, individual or corporation.** §37.15(a): *"Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board."* No corporation threshold appears in the text. [S2] The Housing Inventory overview says: *"All owners of residential property are required to report"* — even if you are not currently a landlord on a particular unit. [S1]

2. **No, you do not "apply" for the license — it is generated automatically.** The Housing Inventory page: *"Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license that allows them to impose annual allowable and banked rent increases"* and *"a rent increase license will be **automatically generated** for non-owner occupied units"*; *"The rent increase license **does not need to be included** with your rent increase notice."* [S1] Widely repeated online claims of a separate license **application** are not supported by the official page.

3. **The license must be current on the effective date, and you should confirm it before you serve (recommended workflow).** §37.15(e): if the landlord has not substantially complied, *"the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance. Upon receipt of the required information … the suspension shall be terminated, and the license … shall be restored prospectively, but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension."* [S2] The Housing Inventory page: *"If you do not submit … by the annual deadline, the Rent Board may notify your tenants that you are not in compliance"* and the banked-increase page: *"a landlord must obtain a rent increase license **before imposing** annual allowable and/or banked rent increases."* [S1][S6]

4. **No petition or Board approval is needed for the ordinary annual or properly banked increase itself.** §37.3(a)(1)–(2) authorizes the annual and banked amounts subject to §37.15; Form 571 says the increase may be imposed *"without filing a petition."* [S3][S4][S5] Petitions are only for the six special categories (capital improvement, O&M, comparable rents, etc.) and must be **filed before the notice** — not relevant here.

**So the order that will not fail a tenant-side review is:** report/update the Inventory with truthful data → retain the submission confirmation and a dated screenshot showing a current license → then calculate, write, and serve the notice. **All steps have a direct official link in the executive summary's 7-step checklist.** No Inventory filing, license request, or tenant notice was performed by this repository.

## 3. What is reasonable and normal in Outer Sunset ZIP 94122 — local/state/federal

### Local law (controls for these units if rent-controlled as reported)
- **1.6% annual allowance** for effective dates **Mar 1 2026–Feb 28 2027** (60% of Bay Area CPI increase, capped at 7%). [S4][S5] One increase per 12 months, on **base rent only**, never rounded up.
- **Banked:** properly banked skipped years are **added** (e.g. 1.4% + 1.6% = 3.0%), not compounded. [S6][S7][S13]
- **Notice:** 30 days at or below 10% (alone or combined with any other increase in the prior 12 months), **90 days above 10%**, +5 days if mailed. [S8][S5]
- **For Unit 1** (tenancy Feb 1 2025, rent $3,995, no increase since start): on a **Feb 1 2027 effective date** exactly 24 months have passed and 12 months since the 1.4% became available — both the banking page's 24-month wording and Rule 4.12's 12-month wording **agree**, so **1.4% (banked) + 1.6% (current) = 3.0%** is the same on either reading. The 3.0% is conditional on no intervening increases — the ledger itself is owner-supplied (F01). [S6][S7][S5 → worked example]
- **For Unit 2** (tenancy Jun 1 2019, rent $3,150, no ledger supplied): **1.8+0.7+2.3+3.6+1.7+1.4 = 11.5% banked + 1.6% current = 13.1%** (full bank) — every skipped year is far beyond both banking thresholds, so the wording conflict does not change the total. The 13.1% **assumes** no intervening increases — that fact was **not established** (F01/F18) and is therefore not presented as an entitlement.

> **"Normal" vs "lawful":** The Rent Board does **not** publish a "normal" or average increase for ZIP 94122. The **lawful annual allowance is itself the norm** for a rent-controlled unit: a **documented 1.6% (or less), at lawful timing, with neutral treatment** is the conservative option a tenant lawyer would find hardest to challenge. Claiming a market "normal" of e.g. 5–8% would be unverified and, for a controlled unit, unlawful to impose. Secondary market datasets (SF Open Data inventory bands, HUD FMR, Census ACS B25064) are not published at ZIP-level with verified values and are therefore **left blank and flagged (F10)**, not estimated.

### State law
- **AB 1482 (Civil Code §1947.12):** the City publishes **8.8%** for AB 1482-covered SF units Aug 1 2026–Jul 31 2027, but expressly states it **does NOT apply** to units subject to San Francisco's local rent limits. [S10][S9(d)(3)] **Do not use 8.8% for these units.**
- **Civil Code §827** sets the 30/90-day notice floors statewide (above); San Francisco's Housing Inventory/license is a **stricter local addition** that AB 1482 explicitly permits via (d)(3). [S8][S9]
- **Costa-Hawkins (§1954.53):** initial rent on a truly vacant covered unit may be set at market, but a **partial change where an original occupant remains is not a vacancy reset** — so the April 2026 change in Unit 1 is not a reset. [S11/S14]

### Federal law
- **No general federal rent ceiling** for private, unsubsidized housing was identified in the sources reviewed; HUD's Fair Housing Act page addresses **discrimination** (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability), not price. Recorded as **F07 absence-of-evidence**, not quoted authority. **Subsidy status is unknown** and could add separate federal/state/local restrictions — verify before acting. [S19][S18]

## 4. How corporations handle increases vs an individual owner

**Legally identical.** Both are "owners" under §37.15/§37.3 with the same reporting, license, calculation, and notice duties. Corporations gain no higher cap and individuals get no exemption. [S2][S3]

**Operationally (recommended, not empirically verified — no official study of actual practice was confirmed, flagged as F17):**

| Corporation (typical) | Individual owner (same compliance, lighter tools) |
|---|---|
| Compliance calendar (Inventory Mar 1 + fee Mar 1; 30-day contact-update rule) | One calendar entry: **Mar 1** Inventory+fee for both units |
| Per-unit ledger (lease, base rent, anniversary, banked balance) | One spreadsheet per unit — the `data/units.json`/`docs/data/units.json` structure is the template |
| Property-management software queuing 30/90-day notices (+5 if mailed) | One evidence folder per unit (lease, every notice, proof of service, ledger, calculation, license screenshot) |
| Outside counsel + uniform notice templates + written business-reason memos | One-hour paid landlord-attorney check on the 6.14 question and the one-vs-both decision + one written file note with the business reason |

Corporations also **may not use algorithmic pricing software** that recommends rents from non-public competitor data in SF. [S16][AB 325] The site's **Owner practices** page lists the shared workflow with a source link for every recommendation and labels empirical-practice claims as unverified.

## 5. How to inform tenants correctly — and what counts as a reasonable reason

**Correct notice — the exact order a tenant lawyer checks:**

1. **Written notice** stating: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, which portion is banked (+ its dates). [S5][S6][S7 §4.10]
2. **Timing:** 30 days if ≤10% (alone or combined with any other increase in the prior 12 months), **90 days if >10%**. [S8(b)(2)-(3)]
3. **Service:** **personally** or **by mail under CCP §1013** — add **5 days** if mailed. Do not assume email/text suffices. [S8(b)(1)][S5]
4. **Compute from the correct rate:** use the percentage **in effect on the effective date**, not the service date. All serve-by dates on this site are for **personal service**; add 5 days if mailing (both columns are shown on the Units page). [S5]
5. **Keep the file:** lease + every notice + proof of service + rent receipts/ledger + calculation sheet + dated license screenshot. A tenant may file a **Form 516C** petition at **no charge with no time limit** to audit the whole rent history, and that file is your answer. [S20]

**Concrete serve-by dates for these units (personal service):**

- **Unit 1 (1.6% or 3.0%, both ≤10%):** for a **Feb 1 2027** effective date → serve by **Jan 2 2027** (mail: Dec 28 2026).
- **Unit 2 (1.6% annual-only):** for Feb 1 2027 → Jan 2 2027; for Feb 28 2027 → Jan 29 2027 (mail: Jan 24).
- **Unit 2 (13.1% full bank, >10%):** for **Feb 28 2027** → serve by **Nov 30 2026** (90 days; mail: Nov 25). [S8]
- Any effective date **on/after Mar 1 2027** needs the **unpublished 2027–28 rate** — deliberately left blank (F06). [S4]

**Reasonable (lawful) reasons:**

- A truthful business explanation such as *"annual allowable increase under the Rent Ordinance; to keep pace with operating costs"* is fine to **state**, but it does **not authorize** exceeding the local allowance. Above-allowance increases need a **separate lawful route** (certified capital improvement / O&M petition filed **before** the notice, etc. — not applicable here). [S3][Landlord petitions]
- No cost narrative bypasses the cap. Routine maintenance alone does not create a lawful above-allowance increase.
- **Unlawful reasons you must never use or invent:** discrimination on **FEHA/HUD** protected traits, **retaliation** for complaints/organizing, or **coercion to get a tenant to leave** (a rent increase "substantially in excess of market rates" is listed as evidence of bad faith). [S18][S19][S17][S15]
- **No provision** was found requiring you to justify the business economics of a within-allowance increase to the Rent Board — but a tenant-practice lawyer will ask whether the reason is neutral and documented.

## 7. One unit or both — discrimination/inquiry risk

**No provision requiring identical or simultaneous increases across units was found** in the sources reviewed. [S3][F08] That absence does **not** make a selective increase lawful — the anti-discrimination and retaliation rules still apply to the **reason** for any difference.

What is prohibited is a difference **because of** a protected characteristic (FEHA: race, national origin, immigration status, source of income, familial status, disability, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, etc.; HUD: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability) or **because of** retaliatory/coercive motive. [S18][S19][S17][S15]

**Applied to these facts:** the two tenancies are in **materially different lawful positions** — one began **2025** (one bankable anniversary, if any), the other **2019** (seven years of potential bank, if the ledger is clean). Treating them **differently because of their anniversary dates and banked balances** is explained by the tenancy history, **not by the tenants**. That documentation is what defeats a discrimination inference.

- **Lowest-scrutiny, most obviously neutral option:** apply the same method to both — e.g. **1.6% annual-only on both units**. The results still differ in dollars because the base rents differ, but the method is identical.
- **Also lawful (with better documentation):** impose a larger conditional amount on one unit where the history supports a larger banked balance, **provided** you write the reason as *"anniversary Jun 2019, banked years 2020–2025 = 11.5%, plus current 1.6%"* — not as a fact about the tenants.
- **Raising only one unit does NOT automatically look discriminatory** if the file note ties the choice to history/timing and the notice/calculation is clean — but selective service **invites closer scrutiny of motive**, so the documentation matters more.

**Recommendation (not legal advice):** before serving anything, **write one paragraph per unit with the business reason tied to anniversary date, published allowance, and documented banked balance**; apply the **same method** to both even if the numeric result differs; preserve that note with the notices. See **Owner practices** for the one-vs-both analysis with sources. [hub/practices.html]
