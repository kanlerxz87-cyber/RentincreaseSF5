Short answer to your first question: yes, an individual owner needs the licence - but there is no application and nothing to send the Rent Board in advance.

Reporting each unit in the Rent Board Housing Inventory automatically generates the rent increase licence. The licence has to exist on the date the increase takes effect. Skip it and the increase is unenforceable and the tenant is not obligated to pay it (SF Admin Code §37.15(e); Rent Board Housing Inventory page). Being an individual rather than a corporation changes nothing in the ordinance. Full detail in step 2 and 3 of the process, and in the executive summary.

What this file says, in four sentences

  1. The two increases are capped at 1.6% of base rent for an effective date between March 1, 2026 and February 28, 2027, and the cap is the same for an individual owner and a corporation.
  2. Unit 1 can lawfully take a 1.6% increase on February 1, 2027 ($63.92); whether it can also add the skipped 2026 increase ($119.85) depends on a genuine conflict between the ordinance text and the Rent Board's website, which is flagged rather than decided.
  3. Unit 2 has six unused years of increases behind it, worth $412.65 including the current year - lawful, but the one item in this file most likely to attract a tenant petition, which is why a held-back 10% option is also priced ($315.00).
  4. Nothing in the reviewed federal, state or local sources requires raising both units, or raising them equally. What is prohibited is discrimination on a protected ground and bad-faith pressure to vacate.
