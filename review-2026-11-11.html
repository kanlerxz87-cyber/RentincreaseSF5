License first: individual owners are covered

Yes—individual owners of covered units need the rent-increase license too. Report through the Housing Inventory; the license is generated automatically. Confirm it is current on the effective date. Ordinary annual and correctly calculated banked increases are not a request for advance approval of each notice. A license cannot prevent other legal challenges. Housing Inventory · Section 37.15 · Banking rules

Beginner’s workflow

  1. Confirm actual unit coverage, fixed-term lease restrictions, concessions, housing services and any subsidy requirements. The reported building year alone does not settle coverage.
  2. Use authentic records to report each unit in the Rent Board Portal linked from the Housing Inventory page; save the confirmation privately. Check current license status before preparing notices.
  3. Authenticate the current lawful base and complete rent ledger. Confirm at least 12 months have elapsed since tenancy start or last effective increase. Do not infer a vacancy reset from a roommate change.
  4. Select an effective date and its published allowance. Annual-only arithmetic here uses 1.6%; recheck for dates after February 28, 2027. Do not use banking until history and pending legislation are resolved.
  5. Prepare a written notice with the amount, percentage and effective date, plus any separately identified banked component. Review statutory service and mailing time, and preserve proof of service.
  6. Apply the same objective decision method to both units while checking each unit’s legal timing individually. Screen for retaliation, discrimination and accommodation issues; retain an evidence file privately.
