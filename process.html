02 · Report annuallyUse the Rent Board Portal at https://portal.sfrb.org/. Reporting deadline is March 1 each year; late reporting remains possible. Keep business-contact details updated within 30 days of changes.Verified ruleHousing Inventory & licensing ↗
03 · Check each licenseConfirm the current unit license is on file and remains effective on the increase date. Retain evidence privately. Housing Inventory reporting is separate from the Rent Board fee exemption process.Verified rule + recordkeeping recommendationHousing Inventory & licensing ↗
04 · Audit rent historyEstablish lawful base rent and the last effective increase. First annual increase follows 12 months from lease start; subsequent annual increases require at least 12 months. A delayed increase changes the anniversary.Verified ruleSF annual increases & notice ↗
05 · Select effective-date rateUse the allowance effective on the increase date, not service date. Apply to base rent including housing services, excluding temporary passthroughs/fluctuating charges; do not round up to whole dollars.Verified ruleSF annual increases & notice ↗
06 · Prepare written noticeState dollar increase, percentage and effective date; recommendation: also show old and new base rent. Identify annual and any banked portions separately; Rule 4.10 requires disclosure of applicable certified work, utilities and RAP portions.Rule + recommendationRent Board Rule 4.10 ↗
07 · Serve properlyFor periodic residential tenancies, §827 allows personal delivery or mail. At least 30 days for an increase totaling 10% or less over the prior 12 months; over 10% generally requires 90 days. These are notice thresholds, not permission to exceed a cap.Verified ruleCivil Code §827 ↗
08 · Allow mailing timeSF guidance says add five days if mailed. Check applicable service rules and actual destination; do not assume email or a text message satisfies statutory service. Fixed-term lease terms need separate review.Verified guidance + recommendationSF annual increases & notice ↗
