# Research: the Rent Board "license to impose rent increases"

**Question asked:** as an individual (non-corporate) owner, do I still need to apply for
the license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants of a rent increase?

**Answer: the license, yes. A petition or application for the annual increase, no. Being
an individual owner is irrelevant.**

## What the ordinance says

SF Administrative Code §37.15(e):

> "If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information
> about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a
> license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections
> 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting
> obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended
> during the period of the landlord's noncompliance. Upon receipt of the required
> information from the landlord, the suspension shall be terminated, and the license to
> impose rent increases shall be restored **prospectively**, but a tenant shall not be
> obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension."
>
> — https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing

§37.3(a)(1) makes the license a precondition of the increase: the operative sentence
reads "A landlord **who has reported the required information about their rental unit to
the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to** impose annually a
rent increase..." — https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-373-rent-limitations

## Who owes it

§37.15(a): "**Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37** shall be
required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board." No size
threshold, no corporate/individual distinction, no exemption for small owners.

The Rent Board's fee page states the same principle for the separate fee obligation:
"All residential property owners must pay a per-unit Rent Board fee unless the unit(s)
have a current exemption. **This includes single-family homes and condominiums**, units
built after 1979, vacant units, and units occupied by friends or family members."
— https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-fee

## How you actually get the license

You do not "apply" for it. You complete the annual Housing Inventory submission and the
license is generated automatically for non-owner-occupied units:

> "If you are a property owner, once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent
> increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units. A
> landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before
> any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect. The rent increase license does
> not need to be included with your rent increase notice."
>
> — https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory

Deadline: **March 1** every year. Late filing carries no monetary penalty, but the
increase is unenforceable without the license on the effective date. Reporting is made
under penalty of perjury and must be updated within 30 days of any change to the owner's
or manager's business contact information.

The Rent Board also implements §37.15 in Part XIII of its Rules and Regulations,
including **Section 13.14, "Licensing – Notice of Rent Increase"**
— https://www.sf.gov/documents/36249/625_RR_Complete_1-13-26.pdf

## Two separate obligations — do not confuse them

| | Housing Inventory report | Rent Board fee |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Ord. 265-20 (§37.15) | Admin. Code Ch. 37A |
| Deadline | March 1 annually | March 1 annually |
| Produces | The rent increase license | Nothing by itself |
| Amount | Free | $59.00/dwelling unit, 2026–27 |
| Penalty | No monetary penalty; increase void | 5%/month, capped at 15% |

"Requesting a Rent Board Fee exemption does not satisfy Rent Board Housing Inventory
reporting requirements."
— https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-rent-board-fee-and-housing-inventory-requirement

## Do you have to "inform the Rent Board" of the increase?

For an annual or banked increase, **no**. No petition, no filing, no copy to the Board.

You must file with the Board *before serving notice* only for: capital improvement
passthroughs; operating & maintenance increases; special circumstances/comparable rents;
Proposition I past rent history; utility passthroughs (petition or worksheet); and
§1.21 (no tenant in occupancy) petitions. Bond passthroughs need a worksheet but not a
petition. — https://sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs

## Consequences

1. Increase unenforceable for the months the license was suspended (§37.15(e)).
2. The Rent Board may notify your tenants that you are not in compliance
   (https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory).
3. The 2027 Informational Notice states: "Some rent increases given without a license
   will be null and void."
   — https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-rent-board-fee-and-housing-inventory-requirement

## Irregularity logged

None of the official pages reviewed state whether **fee delinquency** suspends the
license. The license is described as flowing from *reporting* only. Flagged, not assumed.
