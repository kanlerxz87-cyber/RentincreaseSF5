## 1. FRONT AND CENTER: the Rent Board "license" question

### Short answer

**Yes — as an individual property owner (not a corporation), you still must comply with the Rent Board's licensing requirement for a rent-controlled unit.** But three parts of what you read need correcting:

1. **It is not a separate "application."** The Rent Increase License is **generated automatically** when you file your annual **Housing Inventory** report for a tenant-occupied unit. Reporting is how you "inform the Rent Board." [sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [SF Admin Code § 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing)
2. **You do not file a notice or petition about the specific increase** for an ordinary annual or banked increase. Form 571 states the allowable annual increase may be imposed "**without filing a petition at the Rent Board**." [Form 571 (official PDF)](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [sf.gov — annual rent increase for multiple years](https://www.sf.gov/information--annual-rent-increase-multiple-years)
3. **The license does not prevent a challenge.** It is a precondition to imposing the increase. Tenants can still file an unlawful-rent-increase petition — **free, with no time limit** — and a Tenant Summary Petition when a notice is clearly unlawful on its face. [sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history/) · [sf.gov — Tenant Summary Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--tenant-summary-petitions/)

**Why it applies to you:** § 37.15(a) is written as "**Owners** of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report…" and § 37.2(h) defines "Landlord" as "an owner, lessor, sublessor … and the agent, representative or successor of any of the foregoing" — there is **no corporate carve-out**. For condominiums and buildings with **fewer than 10 residential units**, reporting (and licensing) has been required **since March 1, 2023**; buildings with 10+ units since July 1, 2022. [§ 37.15 text](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [§ 37.2 definitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-372-definitions) · [sf.gov Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [Ord. 265-20 announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--new-legislation-creates-housing-inventory-and-requires-owners-obtain-license-imposing)

**Timing nuance that matters most:** sf.gov states "**A current Rent Increase License must be on file with the San Francisco Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect**," and Rent Board Rule 13.14 says the **effective date, not the date of service**, is what counts. So the license must be current on the day the increase takes effect — it is not enough to have applied before notifying the tenant, and it is not enough to obtain it afterwards. [sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory) · [Rules & Regulations Part XIII, § 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent)

**Consequence if you skip it:** "Any rent increase … purporting to be effective while the landlord is unlicensed **shall render the entire rent increase null and void**" (Rule 13.14). § 37.15(e) adds that "a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension," and sf.gov warns the tenant may be entitled to a refund (possibly for multiple years). [§ 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent) · [§ 37.15(e)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [sf.gov noncompliance notice](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory)

### Step-by-step, in the right order

| # | Step | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Confirm coverage.** Units built on or before June 13, 1979 that are not used as a single-family dwelling "may have both" rent control and eviction protection. Verify the build date on the official Property Information Map and rule out condo / substantial-rehab / new-construction / government-regulated exemptions. | [sf.gov — Learn about SF rental laws](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws/) · [Property Information Map](https://sfplanninggis.org/PIM/) |
| 2 | **Report into the Housing Inventory by March 1 each year.** Report under penalty of perjury: owner/manager business contact, business registration number, approximate square footage, bedrooms/bathrooms, occupancy or vacancy start date, 12-month occupancy history, base rent in $250 increments, and whether utilities are included. Owner contact information becomes public. | [sf.gov Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [§ 37.15(b)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) |
| 3 | **Confirm the Rent Increase License is active** for the unit and the relevant year in the [Rent Board Portal](https://portal.sfrb.org/) (search by address or APN). It is automatically generated for non-owner-occupied units after reporting. Late reports are accepted, but "your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice." | [sf.gov Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [sf.gov noncompliance notice](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory) |
| 4 | **Pay the annual Rent Board fee separately** ($59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026-2027, due March 1). Paying the fee does not replace reporting; reporting does not exempt the fee. 50% ($29.50) may be recovered from a qualifying tenant. | [sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-fee) |
| 5 | **Calculate the amount.** Use the percentage **in effect on the effective date** (currently **1.6%**, 3/1/2026–2/28/2027), applied to **base rent only**, once every 12 months, with no compounding and **no rounding up**. | [sf.gov — Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Current Rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit) · [2026 announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) |
| 6 | **Serve a written notice**: 30 days for an increase of 10% or less (add 5 days if mailed), 90 days if the increase alone or combined within 12 months exceeds 10%. Include the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and (for banked increases) which part is banked and the years/dates it is based on. The license does **not** need to be attached. | [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV) · [sf.gov — Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [sf.gov — Banked rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [§§ 37.3(b), 4.10, 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) |
| 7 | **On the effective date, the license must still be current.** Nonconforming increases are "null and void" (§ 37.3(b)(5)). Keep the lease, notices, proof of service, license confirmation and calculation sheet. | [§ 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent) · [§ 37.3(b)(5)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) |

**Exception:** six categories of increase **do** require a landlord petition **before** serving the notice — capital improvements, operating & maintenance expenses, comparable-rent special circumstances, Proposition I past rent history, utility passthrough, and a Rule 1.21 tenant-in-occupancy determination. [sf.gov — Landlord Petitions and Passthroughs](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs)

**Free help:** Rent Board counseling is free for landlords and tenants at 415-252-4600, but counselors cannot give legal advice. [sf.gov — Speak with a Rent Board counselor](https://www.sf.gov/speak-rent-board-counselor/)

## 2. What is a "reasonable and normal" increase under local, state and federal law?

- **Local (controlling):** The only increase that official sources describe as available without a petition is the Rent Board's published annual allowable increase: **1.6% of base rent, effective March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027**, once every 12 months. It is citywide — the Rent Board does **not** publish a 94122-specific rate. [Current Rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit) · [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco)
- **State:** California's AB 1482 cap (8.8% in SF for 8/1/2026–7/31/2027) **does not apply** to units already subject to SF's stricter rent increase limits — Civil Code § 1947.12(d)(3) exempts them. Civil Code § 827's 30/90-day notice rules still apply. [sf.gov — AB 1482](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482) · [Civil Code § 1947.12](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12.&lawCode=CIV) · [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV)
- **Federal:** No official federal source imposing a numeric cap on private, unsubsidized rents was located — **that value is left blank and flagged**, not inferred. Federal law that does apply is the Fair Housing Act's ban on discriminatory rental terms (42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)). [HUD](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview) · [42 U.S.C. § 3604 (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2010-title42/pdf/USCODE-2010-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.pdf)

**Applied to these two units** (arithmetic on owner-provided rents + official rates; not an official determination):

| Unit | Owner-provided base rent | 1.6% annual increase | New base rent | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | $3,995.00 (Feb 1, 2025; continuing at $3,995.00 as of April 1, 2026) | **$63.92** | **$4,058.92** | 30 days (+5 if mailed) |
| Unit 2 | $3,150.00 (June 1, 2019) | **$50.40** | **$3,200.40** | 30 days (+5 if mailed) |

Unit 2 also has six skipped anniversary years (2020–2025). Adding them gives **13.1% / $412.65 → $3,562.65** under Rule 4.12's 12-month banking language, or **11.7% / $368.55 → $3,518.55** under the current sf.gov page's 24-month language. **These two official sources conflict** — this is the single most important item on the Flagged for Review page. Either version exceeds 10% and triggers a **90-day** notice plus a banked-amount breakdown. [Rule 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring) · [sf.gov — Banked rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV)

**Recommendation (analysis, not an official value):** the conservative, easiest-to-defend action is a single 1.6% annual increase per unit at the correct anniversary date, with a current license and compliant notice.

## 3. Corporations vs. individual owners

The Rent Ordinance and the licensing rules make **no entity-type distinction**: the same 1.6% cap, 12-month rule, and § 37.15 license apply to every "landlord." The official differences that do exist are by **building size** (10+ units reported from 7/1/2022; condos and 1–9 unit buildings from 3/1/2023; bulk upload offered for 5+ units) and one state-law rule: AB 1482's single-family/condo exemption is **unavailable** to a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. Above-cap increases require the same documented Rent Board petition from any landlord. [§ 37.2(h)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-372-definitions) · [§ 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [Civil Code § 1947.12(d)(5)(A)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12.&lawCode=CIV) · [Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs)

**No official government source describes internal corporate rent-setting practices, algorithms, or portfolio-wide policies.** That cell is left blank and flagged rather than filled from commercial blogs.

## 4. Correct way to inform tenants, and reasonable reasons

Written notice, 30/90 days (plus 5 if mailed), stating the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and any banked portion with the dates it is based on; components (annual, banked, capital improvement, O&M, utility, RAP loan) must be broken out. [§ 37.3(b)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) · [Rule 4.10/4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring) · [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco)

Legitimate reasons: the annually published allowable increase (**no justification or petition required**); previously banked skipped increases (with breakdown); a successful petition based on documented capital improvements, O&M costs, comparable rents, or utility passthroughs; or 50% of the Rent Board fee. **Not** legitimate: a new roommate/occupant, a market-rate reset while an original occupant remains, retaliation, or any protected characteristic. [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [sf.gov — Roommates and subletting](https://www.sf.gov/roommates-and-subletting) · [§ 37.3(a)(11), (d)(2)(B)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) · [Rule 6.13/6.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-vi-rent-increase-justifications)

## 5. Tenant-advocate and tenant-lawyer scrutiny

San Francisco tenants have: a **free** unlawful-rent-increase petition with **no time limit** and an ALJ hearing; a Tenant Summary Petition for facially unlawful notices; **free legal assistance** through the Tenant Right to Counsel program / Eviction Defense Collaborative; free Rent Board counseling; and an official referral listing of tenant advocates and legal-aid offices (including a Sunset office in 94122). The Rent Board does not adjudicate discrimination or retaliation — those go to the SF Human Rights Commission. [sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history/) · [Tenant and Landlord Resources](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-and-landlord-resources) · [Rent Board Referral Listing](https://www.sf.gov/reports--january-2025--rent-board-referral-listing) · [Housing Trust Fund Five-Year Report (TRC)](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/120882_-_Housing_Trust_Fund_Five-Year_Report_-_2018-2023.pdf) · [About the Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board/about)

**Design target (analysis):** an increase that is exactly the published rate, on the correct anniversary date, on base rent only, with a license confirmed current on the effective date, a fully compliant notice, and a written unit-neutral policy is the version that is independently checkable against every official requirement. No official source can guarantee a particular advocate's or lawyer's opinion, so that outcome is left blank and flagged.

## 6. One unit or both?

**Both is not required by any official source, and one unit alone is not inherently unlawful.** The Rent Ordinance is tenancy-specific: different lease start dates, base rents, anniversary dates and banked histories legitimately produce different amounts. What is unlawful is choosing which unit to increase, or by how much, **because of a protected characteristic or in retaliation** (42 U.S.C. § 3604(b); Government Code § 12955(a), (f)). The defensible approach is one written, uniformly applied, unit-neutral policy plus a documented objective reason for each amount. [sf.gov — Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [sf.gov — Increasing rents during irregular periods](https://www.sf.gov/information--increasing-rents-during-irregular-periods) · [Government Code § 12955](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=12955.&lawCode=GOV) · [HUD](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview)

**Unit 1 specific warning:** because an original occupant still lives there, the April 1, 2026 occupant change does **not** allow a market-rate reset, and an increase based on the new occupant would be unlawful. [§ 37.3(d)(2)(B) and (a)(11)(A)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) · [Rule 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins](https://www.sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-section-614-and-costa-hawkins)

## 7. Irregularities and unverified items

The site's **Flagged for Review** page lists 15 items with source links. The most material:

1. **12-month vs 24-month banking conflict** between Rule 4.12(a) and the current sf.gov banking page — changes Unit 2's maximum.
2. **Conflicting license start dates** between § 37.15/Rule 13.14 (2022/2023) and official Form 538 (2020/2021).
3. **Different remedies** for an unlicensed increase in § 37.15(e) vs Rule 13.14.
4. **2027–2028 rate not published**; the Rent Board has documented a CPI-data/methodology issue for 2026 and 2027 rates.
5. The Rent Board's own site says its online Ordinance text **"is not the official record."**
6. **Owner-provided facts** (build year, coverage, unit count, license status, lease/occupancy dates, prior increases) were not independently verified; the site shows blank cells where an official value is required.
7. **Values intentionally left blank**: federal rent cap for private housing, 94122 market benchmark, official model notice wording, record-retention period, license status, building unit count, new-tenancy question for Unit 1, prior-increase question, "raise both units" claim, guarantee of a lawyer's approval, internal corporate rent-setting practices, and the specific list of additional SF-local protected classes beyond state law.
8. **One verification pass could not reach a PDF section**: the City's official Analysis of Impediments lists a section "Other State/Local Protected Classes", but only the first 30 pages of that PDF were retrievable, so no specific local protected class is asserted from it. An earlier draft's list ("creed, height, place of birth, weight") was removed as unverified.

**A note on third-party guidance:** several commercial landlord guides say an owner must "apply for" a license, that the notice comes first, or that a 10%+ increase needs 60 days. Official sources say the license is automatically generated, no petition is needed, the license must be current on the effective date, and a >10% increase needs 90 days. No non-official source was used to verify any fact in this project.
