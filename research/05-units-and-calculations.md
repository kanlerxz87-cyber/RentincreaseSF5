## 1. Unit 1

**Owner-provided facts:** last lease start **February 1, 2025 @ $3,995/month**; initial occupants Occupant A, Occupant B, Occupant C (placeholders); continuing tenancy current occupants **Occupant A and Occupant D**, **April 1, 2026 @ $3,995/month**.

**Verified legal consequences of the occupant change:**

- § 37.3(d)(2)(B): the Costa-Hawkins/sublet provisions "shall not apply to partial changes in occupancy … where one or more of the occupants … remains an occupant in lawful possession." An original occupant (Occupant A) still resides there, so **no market-rate reset**. [sf.gov § 37.3](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations)
- § 37.3(a)(11)(A) / Rule 6.13 / sf.gov: "Landlords in San Francisco cannot charge more rent simply because a new roommate … has moved into a unit. This is illegal, no matter what the lease itself says." [sf.gov roommates](https://www.sf.gov/roommates-and-subletting) · [Rule 6.13](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-vi-rent-increase-justifications)
- Rule 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins market-rate increases require **all** original occupants to have vacated, plus a timely written 6.14 notice. [sf.gov § 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins](https://www.sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-section-614-and-costa-hawkins)
- § 37.3(d)(1)(A)(ii): where a preceding tenancy was terminated by a § 1946.1 notice or a change in terms noticed under Civil Code § 827, rent increase limitations "continue to apply for the duration of the new tenancy." [sf.gov § 37.3](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) · [Civil Code § 1954.53](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1954.53.&lawCode=CIV)

**Timing:** first annual increase no earlier than 12 months after the lease began → **February 1, 2026**; rate then **1.4%** (3/1/2025–2/28/2026). For any effective date from **March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027**, the rate is **1.6%**. Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the date of service. A later effective date creates a new anniversary date. [sf.gov rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [Current Rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)

Banked percentages are **added, not compounded or prorated**, and are multiplied by the current base rent; no rounding up; no approval needed but they must be calculated correctly; banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant. [sf.gov banked increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [Rule 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring) · [sf.gov multi-year](https://www.sf.gov/information--annual-rent-increase-multiple-years)

| Scenario | Basis | Banked | + Annual | Total | Increase | New base rent | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Rule 4.12(a): "A full 12 months must have elapsed" | 11.5% | 1.6% | **13.1%** | **$412.65** | **$3,562.65** | 90 days |
| B | sf.gov banked page: "at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase" | 10.1% | 1.6% | **11.7%** | **$368.55** | **$3,518.55** | 90 days |
| Conservative | Current annual only | — | 1.6% | 1.6% | **$50.40** | **$3,200.40** | 30 days |

Arithmetic: 1.8+0.7+2.3+3.6+1.7+1.4 = 11.5%; 11.5+1.6 = 13.1%; $3,150 × .131 = $412.65. Scenario B excludes the June 1, 2025 1.4% (only 12 months elapsed by June 1, 2026): 10.1+1.6 = 11.7%; $3,150 × .117 = $368.55.

Both A and B exceed 10% → **90 days' written notice** under Civil Code § 827(b)(3)(A), plus 5 days if mailed, and the notice must identify the banked portion and the dates it is based on. [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV) · [sf.gov banked increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [§ 37.3(b)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) · [Rule 4.12(b)](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring)

**Flag:** the 12/24-month conflict is material — do not serve a banked-increase notice before a Rent Board counselor and counsel resolve it. Also, an off-anniversary effective date can permanently lose an increase and creates a new anniversary date; an effective date on/after 3/1/2027 needs the unpublished 2027-28 rate. [sf.gov worked examples](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [irregular periods](https://www.sf.gov/information--increasing-rents-during-irregular-periods) · [Rent Board CPI memo](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Memo_re_Change_to_1.12_-_2.pdf)

## 3. "Reasonable and normal" conclusion

**Analysis (not an official determination):** the published annual allowable increase — **1.6%** for effective dates 3/1/2026–2/28/2027 — applied once per 12 months on base rent, with a current license and compliant written notice, is the only increase official sources describe as available without a petition or approval, and it is identical for every covered unit in the City regardless of ZIP code. Banked increases are lawful only with exact arithmetic and full disclosure, and are the most likely target of a tenant petition. Anything above the published rate requires a Rent Board petition and approval.
