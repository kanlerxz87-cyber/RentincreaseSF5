# Practice, Notice, Reasons, and Tenant-Side Review

Companion to `data/practice.json` and `data/discrimination.json`. "Analysis" rows are editorial synthesis of verified rules, not official determinations; "unverified" rows are blank in the data.

## 1. How large real-estate corporations handle increases (what can be verified)

Official sources do **not** describe internal corporate rent-setting practices. What *is* verified:

- The definition of "landlord" in § 37.2(h) has **no corporate carve-out**: "An owner, lessor, sublessor, who receives or is entitled to receive rent … and the agent, representative or successor of any of the foregoing." [sf.gov § 37.2](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-372-definitions)
- The same 1.6% cap, 12-month rule and § 37.15 license apply to every landlord of a covered unit. [sf.gov rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [§ 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing)
- The only size-based licensing difference is by **building size**: 10+ unit buildings reported from 7/1/2022; condos and 1–9 unit buildings from 3/1/2023; bulk Excel upload offered for 5+ units, with small owners directed to the "Individual Unit" form. [§ 37.15(c)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)
- One genuine entity-type difference exists in **state** law: AB 1482's single-family/condo exemption is unavailable to a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. It is irrelevant to an SF rent-controlled multi-unit building. [Civil Code § 1947.12(d)(5)(A)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12.&lawCode=CIV) · [sf.gov AB 1482](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482)
- Any landlord seeking more than the annual/banked amount must petition **before** serving the notice, with supporting documentation; O&M petitions require actual costs and proof of payment, and can be defeated where the owner failed to perform ordinary repairs requested by the tenant. [Landlord Petitions and Passthroughs](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs) · [Rule 6.10/6.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-vi-rent-increase-justifications)
- **BLANK / UNVERIFIED:** internal corporate pricing algorithms, portfolio-wide increase policies, renewal software, and similar practices. No official government source was located; commercial guides were excluded.

## 2. How individual owners handle increases (verified routine)

Report into the Housing Inventory by March 1 → pay the Rent Board fee → confirm the license in the Portal → apply the published rate on each unit's anniversary date on base rent only → serve a compliant written notice → keep the records. Every step is individually verified: [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory), [Rent Board Fee](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-fee), [noncompliance notice](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory), [sf.gov rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco), [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV), [tenant petition page](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history/).

Free help for owners: Rent Board counseling is free for landlords and tenants but "cannot give legal advice"; the City also lists the SF Apartment Association, Small Property Owners of San Francisco, and Bar Association conflict-intervention services. [Speak with a Rent Board counselor](https://www.sf.gov/speak-rent-board-counselor/) · [Tenant and Landlord Resources](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-and-landlord-resources) · [Rent Board Referral Listing](https://www.sf.gov/reports--january-2025--rent-board-referral-listing)

## 4. Reasonable reasons

- **Annual allowable increase:** no justification and no petition required. [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf), [sf.gov multi-year](https://www.sf.gov/information--annual-rent-increase-multiple-years)
- **Banked increases:** previously skipped allowable increases, with the years/dates identified. [sf.gov banked increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases)
- **Documented costs:** capital improvements, O&M increases (actual costs, proof of payment), comparable rents, utility passthroughs, Proposition I rent history — all via petition. [Landlord Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs), [Rule 6.10](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-vi-rent-increase-justifications)
- **Rent Board fee:** 50% of the fee paid may be recovered from a qualifying tenant. [Rent Board Fee](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-fee)
- **Not a reason:** a new or additional occupant; a market-rate reset while an original occupant remains; retaliation; any protected characteristic. [§ 37.3(a)(11)(A), (d)(2)(B)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations), [sf.gov roommates](https://www.sf.gov/roommates-and-subletting), [Government Code § 12955](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=12955.&lawCode=GOV)

## 5. Tenant-friendly advocate / lawyer review

- Free unlawful-rent-increase petition, **no time limit**, ALJ hearing with evidence and cross-examination; no lawyer required. [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history/)
- Tenant Summary Petition when a notice is clearly unlawful on its face. [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/information--tenant-summary-petitions/)
- **Free lawyers:** Tenant Right to Counsel (2018 No Eviction Without Representation Act) via the Eviction Defense Collaborative; official City notice states tenants "are eligible for free assistance through the Tenant Right to Counsel Program." [MOHCD Housing Trust Fund Report](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/120882_-_Housing_Trust_Fund_Five-Year_Report_-_2018-2023.pdf) · [sf.gov Tenant and Landlord Resources](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-and-landlord-resources) · [official 1007 notice](https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2023-08/1007%20Notice%20to%20Tenant%20Req%27d%20by%2037.%209(c)-Eng-Sp-Ch-Viet-Rus-Tag%5B74%5D.pdf)
- Tenant counselors and advocates listed officially, including a Sunset office in 94122; the Rent Board does not handle discrimination/retaliation. [Tenant and Landlord Resources](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-and-landlord-resources) · [Referral Listing](https://www.sf.gov/reports--january-2025--rent-board-referral-listing) · [About the Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board/about)
- **Analysis:** the items a tenant-side reviewer will test are coverage, license on the effective date, base rent, rate on the effective date, 12-month spacing, notice timing and contents, banked arithmetic, the additional-occupant prohibition, and discrimination/retaliation — each with its own verified row on the Requirements page. No official source can guarantee a particular lawyer's opinion; that cell is blank and flagged.

## 6. One unit or both?

Verified: the ordinance is tenancy-specific (anniversary date, base rent, banked history differ by unit), so different amounts are a normal consequence; a tenant-side challenge focuses on compliance, not symmetry. Unlawful: choosing the unit or the amount because of a protected characteristic or retaliation. [sf.gov rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [increasing rents during irregular periods](https://www.sf.gov/information--increasing-rents-during-irregular-periods) · [sf.gov banked increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2010-title42/pdf/USCODE-2010-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.pdf) · [Government Code § 12955](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=12955.&lawCode=GOV)

**BLANK / UNVERIFIED:** any official statement that raising both units is required or that it prevents a discrimination claim. Recommended approach (analysis): one written, uniformly applied, unit-neutral policy, with a documented objective reason for each amount, plus a complete compliance file for each unit.
