# Research: state and federal layers

## California Civil Code §827 — notice of a rent increase

- Increase of **10% or less** (alone, or combined with any other increase in the 12
  months before the effective date): **30 days'** written notice.
- Increase **greater than 10%**: **90 days'** written notice.
- Service under CCP §1013 (mail) adds **5 days**.

https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV

The Rent Board states the same rule: "If the notice is mailed, the landlord must give the
tenant 5 more days." — https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco/

Rent Board Form 537 restates it and confirms it applies to 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins
increases too. — https://www.sf.gov/documents/7737/537_6.14_Costa-Hawkins_Petition_3.25.pdf

## California Civil Code §1947.12 — AB 1482 statewide rent cap

- Cap = **5% + CPI**, or **10%, whichever is lower**.
- **No more than two increases in any 12-month period.**
- **(d)(3) exemption:** does not apply to "housing subject to rent or price control
  through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power … that restricts annual
  increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a)."
- **(d)(5)(A)(ii):** the single-family/condo exemption is unavailable to "a
  corporation" (and to a REIT, and to an LLC with at least one corporate member).

https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12&lawCode=CIV

The Rent Board publishes the current San Francisco figure and the interaction:

> "The maximum rent increase for units under AB 1482 from August 1, 2026 to July 31,
> 2027 is 8.8% in San Francisco. This does NOT apply for units under the rent increase
> limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance."
>
> — https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482/

1.6% < 8.8%, so §1947.12(d)(3) disapplies the state cap entirely for these units.

## Costa-Hawkins — Civil Code §§1954.50–1954.535

Vacancy decontrol framework. https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1954.53&lawCode=CIV

The San Francisco application matters more than the statute for this property:

### §6.14 (Rules and Regulations)

Unlimited increase on remaining occupants when the **last original occupant vacates**,
but only if the landlord **timely served** each subsequent occupant a written "6.14
notice" — generally within 60 days of learning of the occupancy. Applies to co-tenants
*and* subtenants/assignees, regardless of move-in date.

### §1954.53(d) / Ordinance §37.3(d)

Unlimited increase to a **subtenant or assignee** who took possession on or after
January 1, 1996. "Any co-tenant who moved in after the original occupant is not subject
to such a rent increase, regardless of when the co-tenant moved in."

Filing a petition for either determination is **optional**.
— https://sf.gov/information/topic-no-329-petition-determination-pursuant-section-614-andor-costa-hawkins

### Single-family home / condominium exemption

Tenancies that commenced on or after January 1, 1996 are exempt from the rent-increase
limits (but not just cause, and not the fee). A single-family dwelling with a legal *or
illegal* in-law unit is a two-unit building and is not exempt; nor is a single-family
dwelling with another residential structure on the same lot.
— https://sf.gov/information/partial-exemption-certain-single-family-homes-and-condominiums-under-costa-hawkins

**This is the single biggest open risk on this file** — both tenancies commenced after
January 1, 1996 (2025 and 2019), so if either unit is an SFR or condo the entire
increase analysis changes. Property type is unverified.

## Additional occupants

"A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate
has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even
if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge."
— https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco/

## Tenant harassment — Ordinance §37.10B

§37.10B(a)(5) covers influencing a tenant to vacate "by means of a rent increase that is
imposed with an intent to defraud, intimidate, or coerce." Evidence of bad faith may
include: "(1) the rent increase was substantially in excess of market rates for
comparable units; (2) the rent increase was within six months after an attempt to
recover possession of the unit; and (3) such other factors as a court or the Rent Board
may deem relevant."

§37.10B(c)(5): treble actual damages **or** $1,000 statutory damages, whichever is
greater, plus attorney's fees and costs to a prevailing plaintiff.
— https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3710b-tenant-harassment

## Algorithmic rent-setting — Ordinance §37.10C

Bans selling or using "revenue management software" that uses non-public competitor data
to set or recommend rents. Up to $1,000 per violation, per month, per unit; tenant right
of action with attorney's fees.
— https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710c-use-and-sale-algorithmic-devices-prohibited

## Fair housing

**Federal (Fair Housing Act).** Race, colour, national origin, religion, sex, familial
status, disability. — https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview

**California (FEHA).** Broader: adds ancestry, citizenship, immigration status, primary
language, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, genetic information,
marital status, **source of income** (including Section 8 vouchers), military/veteran
status, and **age**. Also prohibits **retaliation** for exercising fair housing rights,
and protects associates of people in protected classes.
— https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/

Note what is *not* protected: tenancy length, relationship with the owner, or having a
below-market rent. A large increase aimed at a long-standing tenant is not
discrimination by itself — but it is the first pattern a tenant-side lawyer will examine.

## Rent control coverage generally

June 13, 1979 first-certificate-of-occupancy cutoff; wholly exempt categories; units with
eviction protection only. — https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws

Ordinance No. 248-24 (effective 11/24/24) extended the new-construction date to
June 13, 1994, **but only if authorized by a future modification or repeal of the
Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act**. The operative cutoff therefore remains June 13, 1979.
— https://www.sf.gov/documents/65403/600_Ordinance_11-02-26_2.pdf
