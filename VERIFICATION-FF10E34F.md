## 2. Licence question — verified verbatim (comes first)

§37.15(a) requires **every** owner of a residential unit subject to Chapter 37 to report; there is **no**
entity-form or portfolio-size threshold. An individual owner is covered exactly like a corporation.
Reporting a tenant-occupied unit into the Housing Inventory **generates the rent-increase licence
automatically** — there is no application form and no approval to wait for. The licence must be current
on the increase's effective date; suspension is prospective only and the tenant owes no increased rent
for suspended months. No petition is needed for an ordinary annual or properly banked increase.

**Conclusion:** an individual owner must be licensed, but there is no separate application. Report into
the Inventory first, confirm the licence is on file, then serve notice.

## 3. Banking wording conflict — documented, does not change either unit

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| R6 (banking page) | Skipped increase banks only after **24 full months** without any increase |
| R7 (Rule 4.12) | **12 months** must elapse from the date the increase could have been imposed |
| R1 (worked example) | Clock runs **12 months from the date each increase became available** |

The worked example (R1) shows a skipped 1.7% available 6/1/2024 is banked, while a 1.4% available
6/1/2025 is lost because under 12 months elapsed — i.e. 12 months from availability. Because the first
increase becomes available 12 months after the tenancy starts, the 24-months-from-tenancy-start wording
and the 12-months-from-availability wording describe the same instant. This reconciliation is **analysis,
not a quoted rule** (flag F04).

Both readings agree on these two units:
- **Unit 1** — tenancy began 2025-02-01; on 2027-02-01 exactly 24 months have passed with no increase and exactly 12 months since the 1.4% became available (2026-02-01). Both tests pass → 1.4% banked + 1.6% annual = **3.0%**.
- **Unit 2** — no increase since 2019-06-01; every skipped year is far beyond both thresholds.
